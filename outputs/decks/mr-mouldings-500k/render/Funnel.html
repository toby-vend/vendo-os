06 · The funnel, stage by stage

Why each fix converts

These are trust mechanics at the decision moment, not persuasion. They remove reasons to doubt the number on the screen.

Stage 1 · Impression → click

The head fix and the snippet

610,376 impressions are already served every month and 98.9% are thrown away. Restoring the head means Google reads the written title and description rather than auto-generating one, and honours the canonical so the clean collection page ranks instead of ?page=2&grid_list. The rating guard also removes a one-star badge generated from a single window-board review.

Stage 2 · Click → engaged session

Seven videos, streaming 31.4 MB

Testimonial videos autoplayed on every product page, streaming 31.4 MB in 38 seconds without the customer scrolling. They competed for bandwidth with the one thing the customer came for — the size and length options — which is why options took 45 seconds to become usable. A CLS of 0.362 also meant the buy box physically moved under the customer's thumb after load.

Stage 3 · Engaged → add to cart

The primary button did nothing

Tapping Add to cart before choosing options produced no request, no message, no scroll and no change of state. This is the worst state in any funnel: a customer at maximum intent presses the main button and the site does not acknowledge it. There is no recovery path because there is nothing to react to.

Alongside it, all five recommendation cards showed £6.68 while the same product read "from £10.34" elsewhere on the page, and resolved to £24.65 once configured. Price is the anchor a customer forms before they configure; that gap reads as bait, not as a range.

Stage 4 · Add to cart → cart

The £0.00 drawer

After a successful add, the drawer's first render showed a blank title, a £0.00 subtotal and "Add £50.00 to reach the £50 minimum" — while the cart itself was correct. It stayed wrong for roughly six seconds, at exactly the moment the customer decides whether to continue or check out.

Stage 5 · Cart → checkout

The minimum-order trap

A basket under £50 passed the whole funnel with the Checkout button enabled, then hit a red banner at checkout: order amount is less than the required minimum of £50. A total loss at the highest-intent point on the site, after the customer has already invested the configuration effort.

22.2% of orders sit under £100, so a meaningful share of baskets approach this threshold. Gating it in the cart turns a dead end into an upsell prompt.

Still upstream

The configurator posts to the cart and receives three rejections before succeeding, so tap-to-drawer measures 9.6 seconds with "Adding…" the only feedback. A watchdog message turns dead time into explained time, but only replacing the configurator removes the wait itself.
