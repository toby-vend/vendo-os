VENDO DIGITAL · INTERNAL TRAINING · JULY 2026

The creative is the targeting.

How paid social works in 2026, and the system we run at Vendo — from concept to launch to analysis. ~45 minutes, questions as we go.

Why we're here

Media buying is no longer the edge

  • Targeting is automated. Broad is the default; there is no skill left in interest targeting.
  • Andromeda (Meta's ad-serving overhaul) now decides distribution by reading the creative itself.
  • Consolidation wins on every platform — pooled conversion data models better.
  • The old hacks are dead. Duplicating ad sets, bid tricks — none of it produces real arbitrage any more.

The edge moved to creative — because most agencies and most in-house teams are still bad at it. That gap is our opportunity, and this deck is the system for exploiting it.

Why we're here

The two facts everything else follows from

Every ad fatigues
No exceptions. The only question is when.
Every ad has a spend ceiling
A maximum lifetime spend it can hold before efficiency dies.

So we run a portfolio: many ads holding spend together, never one or two heroes. Three ads at a blended 4x beats one ad at 4x every day — any one can fatigue and the others pick up the slack.

The cautionary tale from the playbook: eight-figure brands built on three winning creatives, all fatiguing at once, arriving 40% down year-on-year. The fix has to happen six months before the problem shows.

Why we're here

Three mistakes we must not make (or let clients make)

  • Activity mistaken for strategy. Volume without a quality bar is junk volume — accounts launching 2,000 ads/month and still unprofitable.
  • Misdiagnosed bottlenecks. Fixing what isn't broken: over-segmenting campaigns when the real problem is not enough top-of-funnel creative.
  • Creative treated as a cost centre. Platforms are just distribution. Creative drives the revenue. Clients who see shoots and assets as a cost to minimise are the ones who stall.

That third one is a client conversation we own. Comprehensive and Dominate include a shoot for a reason — additional shoots (£750 + VAT) are not an upsell for our benefit, they are the growth lever.

PART 01

How Meta actually works now

If the team shares one mental model of the algorithm, every downstream decision — structure, testing, reporting — gets easier.

The algorithm

Andromeda reversed the flow

Before

You chose the audience (interests, lookalikes) and loaded creative underneath it. Targeting was a media-buying skill.

Now

You load ads with no targeting. Meta reads the creative — transcript, copy, visuals, conversion history — and finds the people for it.

The creative you load in IS the targeting. A vague ad ("Hey everyone, look at this!") gives Meta nothing to target with. An ad clearly speaking to one person tells Meta exactly who to find.

This is also why the opening line matters mechanically, not just creatively: Meta auto-transcribes every ad, so what the hook says steers who sees it.

The algorithm

Similar creatives get bundled — and bundles decay

  • Slight variants of the same ad (same shoot, small script change, different format saying the same thing) get bundled and served to the same pool.
  • Result: frequency rises, reach falls, ROAS declines. You stop meeting new people.
  • Meta's own research: purchase probability collapses after two impressions of the same ad. A novel creative as the third touch keeps it high.
> 2.0
Frequency on a cold campaign ad = fatigue red flag. Check your accounts' last 30–60 days this week.
Diversity
The fix is always the same: more genuinely different concepts, not more variants of the winner.
The algorithm

The last-click trap

Meta deliberately sequences ads across a journey — ad 1 warms, ad 2 educates, ad 3 closes — but reports all revenue on the last click.

→

Turning off ads 1 and 2 because they "aren't performing" destroys the sequence that made ad 3 work. Never switch off an ad inside an ad set that's hitting KPI.

PART 02

Concepts: persona × angle × offer

The unit of creative strategy isn't "an ad" or "a format". It's a concept — and diversity means changing at least one of its three parts.

Concepts

A concept has three levers (and a weak fourth)

Persona

Who we're talking to — and who appears on screen. Changing it reaches a different pool.

Angle

The specific argument: which problem we agitate, what proof we bring, what belief we challenge.

Offer

Product + value framing: consultation, bundle, finance option, guarantee, packaging of price.

Format (video / static / carousel) is a fourth, weaker lever — it reaches some new people, but mostly the same ones. It comes last, never first.

Structural rule that follows: one concept per ad set — targeting lives at ad-set level, and mixed concepts both confuse targeting and make results unreadable.

Concepts

Personas are problems, not demographics

"Women 25–45" is not a persona. A persona is an archetype defined by problems, desires and buying triggers — and hyper-specific beats broad every time.

Dental (illustrative)

  • The professional who hides their smile on video calls and has been "thinking about Invisalign for two years"
  • The 60+ denture wearer who has stopped eating out because of loose dentures
  • The nervous patient who hasn't seen a dentist in a decade and is embarrassed to book

Ecommerce (illustrative)

  • The site manager buying workwear for a crew who's sick of kit that fails in winter
  • The tradesperson who buys on durability-per-pound, not on brand

Process: build 2–4 core personas per client manually, drill into pain points, then use AI to expand — but only after the manual work, or you get the same average output as every competitor. Each client's persona list lives in their creative strategy doc.

Concepts

Writing the angle properly for a video means writing the whole script. For a static it compresses to a few sentences. Pick elements per persona, then weave.

Concepts

→
2 · OFFER

The classic failure sequence: test formats first, then swap personas ("we need new audiences") while the messaging is still bad, then discount the offer, and never fix the angle. If an account is underperforming, start at the angle.

PART 03

Hooks

80% of viewers never get past the first three seconds. The hook is where disproportionate effort pays.

Hooks

Where the effort goes

80%
of viewers never watch past the hook. Most people only ever see those three seconds.
15 + 2
15 hooks on 2 bodies beats 2 hooks on 5 bodies. The available performance delta lives in the hook.

Grade every hook on five criteria

  • Clarity — a stranger understands within 3 seconds
  • Relevance — agitate the actual problem; persona call-outs ("if you're a mum of two…") are overrated
  • Novelty — "has this been done a million times?" If yes, don't
  • Specificity — numbers, names, quantified outcomes, right out of the gate
  • Credibility — usually carried by the visual: the right person on screen

A hook has three layers — visual, audio, copy — and you don't need 10/10 on all five criteria. Two done well makes a good hook.

Hooks

Nine hook types to draw from

TypeShape of it
Problem agitation"Still hiding your teeth in photos?" — strong, but only for problem-aware viewers
Contrarian truth"Most of what you've heard about [X] is wrong" — works at every awareness level
Specific proofA measurable outcome with numbers and a timeframe
Curiosity gap"What [group] know about [topic] that you don't"

Sensory / ASMRNo words; texture, close-ups, product handling — currently strong in fashion/product

Social proofLead with the credibility marker itself

At least seven of these apply to every client we run. The examples above are shapes, not scripts — the persona work decides the words.

Hooks

Don't ruin the hook with the bridge

The bridge is the transition from hook to body. The classic failure: a great hook, then the product introduced immediately — the viewer feels the sell and leaves.

  • Move viewers through awareness stages: unaware → problem aware → solution aware → product aware — across the ad, not in one jump.
  • Introduce the product as late as possible. Later introduction = fewer but far more qualified viewers = more total conversions, not fewer.
  • Retention-curve thinking ("get the product in before people drop off") is exactly backwards.

The playbook's benchmark example introduces the product ~90 seconds into the ad — and that is precisely why it converts and scales.

Hooks

Hook swaps are nearly free spend

  • A new hook = a new creative ID to Meta — it isn't bundled with the original and reaches a fresh audience.
  • The playbook's result: fatigued winners with 50 new hooks rotated on delivered another $100K of spend at the same efficiency — for about an hour of shooting.
  • Failed ads with bad hook rates can be revived by fixing only the hook.

What this means for our shoots

Every videography shoot must capture multiple hook takes per concept — a few extra minutes on the day multiplies every asset we cut for months afterwards. This goes into the shot list as standard, not as a nice-to-have.

PART 04

Formats

Concept first, format last — and some strong opinions the data backs up.

Formats

Statics are wildly underrated

  • Cheap and fast: use statics to rapid-test angles, then develop winners into video — or simply go hard on statics because the cost/return equation works.
  • "Statics only work bottom-of-funnel" is a skill issue — brands run six figures a day on top-of-funnel images.
  • Judge formats by cost per ad vs contribution per ad, not by absolute performance. A £40 static that produces less than a £500 video can still be the better investment.
  • Long-form-copy statics (image + a story that walks the reader through awareness stages) crush with older Facebook demographics — which is exactly the dental patient base.

Advertorial-style statics ("the best X of 2026") only work paired with an education-led landing page — the ad and the destination are one system.

Formats

Video, partnership ads, and a DPA warning

  • Educational long-form / VSL-style video performs strongly with older demographics — it moves people through every awareness stage. Very relevant to dental treatments that need education (implants, orthodontics).
  • Partnership ads (running through a creator's or clinician's profile alongside the brand) borrow the profile's audience for cold targeting and don't feel like ads. For dental: content through the practice's and clinician's own pages is our version of this.
  • DPA warning (ecommerce): attributed ROAS flatters catalogue ads heavily — check incremental attribution, often ~50% lower. Shifting budget into DPAs starves the top of funnel and eventually topples the account.

Production mix, not campaign budgets

Funnel allocation now happens through what we make: roughly 70–90% of assets top-of-funnel, ~20% middle, ~5–10% bottom.

At our typical client budgets (£1–3K/month), the playbook's answer is few concepts done properly, not many done thinly — with 60–80% of spend effectively testing.

Never turn a winner off to relaunch it in a scaling campaign. Winning ad sets are untouchable — new ads for a winning concept go in a new ad set ("concept one — shoot two").

Testing

Metric reliability, in order

#MetricHow to use it
1Amount spentCounterintuitive but the best long-run proxy — Meta funds what's working in the sequence, even when last-click hides it.
2CPA / CPL / ROASOnly on 7-day click or incremental attribution, at ad-set/campaign level. For dental: cost per lead and cost per booked consult from GHL.

Volume floor from the playbook: roughly 1 new ad per £1,000 of monthly spend — a £3K dental client needs at least 3 genuinely new ads a month, before hook variants. One shoot, cut properly (hooks × formats × concepts), covers months of this.

PART 06

Fatigue & portfolio management

Squeeze or stretch — but always know which you're doing, and why.

Fatigue

Shape the curve deliberately

Every ad's lifetime spend is fixed-ish — but you choose the shape: squeeze it hard and it dies in six weeks; run it gently and it lives six months.

  • Squeeze when the client needs volume now (capacity to fill, stock to shift).
  • Stretch when the pipeline is full or production of new creative is behind.
  • Small personas have hard daily ceilings and are churning audiences — rotate creative rather than driving frequency up and burning the pool.

When a creative fatigues (in order of usefulness)

  • Rotate new hooks onto it
  • Translate it into another format (video → static, static → long-copy)
  • Accept it: fatigue tactics only squeeze the last bit out — the portfolio is the real protection
Portfolio

What we make, in what proportions

50–60%
Winning-content replication — hook iterations, format translations, redoing what worked. The highest-leverage content type, and the one everyone under-does.
20–30%
Iterations — fixing underperformers, adapting working concepts to new personas or offers.
20–30%
Net-new concepts — essential (everything fatigues) but the most commonly overweighted.

And shift the emotional mix: most brand content is ~90/10 desire-led vs pain-led. The best performance mix is nearer 60/40 pain-led.

ABO vs CBO is a risk decision: CBO concentrates spend into the top ~4% of ads (max efficiency, max fragility). Steady state should be the de-risked position; CBO temporarily when a client needs a short-term efficiency spike.
