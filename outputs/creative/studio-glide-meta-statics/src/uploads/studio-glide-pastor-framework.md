# PASTOR Framework: Studio Glide Pilates

**Client:** Studio Glide Pilates
**Prepared by:** Vendo Digital
**Date:** 30 July 2026
**Source:** Studio Glide Pilates Audience Intelligence Report, Vendo Digital, 30 July 2026

---

## How to use this document

PASTOR is a persona-level tool. One PASTOR written to cover five segments produces generic copy, which is the exact failure the Audience Intelligence Report warns against. This document therefore contains:

- **Five complete PASTOR builds**, one per segment
- **One composite PASTOR** for the Oxted priority list page, which has to address several segments at once

**The framework, as applied here:**

| Letter | Stands for | The job |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | Person, Problem, Pain | Name who she is and state her problem in her own words |
| **A** | Amplify and Aspiration | What happens if nothing changes, and what she actually wants |
| **S** | Story and Solution | The mechanism, and why this studio specifically |
| **T** | Transformation and Testimony | The after state, backed by proof |
| **O** | Offer | Exactly what she gets, stated plainly |
| **R** | Response | One clear ask |

**Structure of each build:** strategy first, then a copy-ready block. Copy blocks can be used as landing page long copy, as a Meta primary text, or as the spine of a video script. Trim from the bottom of A and T when you need length reduction, never from P.

**Location:** Segments 1 to 4 are written location-agnostic with `[STUDIO]` placeholders so they run at Banstead and Oxted. Segment 5 is Oxted only.

---

## Two constraints that apply to every build below

**1. The Testimony section is currently unwritable.** There are approximately 185 five star Google reviews and not one appears in owned marketing. Every T section below carries a `[TESTIMONIAL]` placeholder. Until these are collected, the T section is the weakest part of every piece of copy on the account, and it is the section that does the most work in this category. This is the highest priority creative asset.

**2. Health claims need substantiation.** Segments 2 and 4 carry the most claim risk (pelvic floor, abdominal separation, bone density, menopause). Where copy below references physiology, it is written as experience or mechanism language rather than outcome promise. Do not harden it without compliance sign off. Meta also restricts creative implying negative self perception of the body, so no before and after, no weight loss framing, no implication that her current body is a problem.

# 1. The Reset Seeker

*Professional woman, 30 to 45. Everything in her life is managed except her own body.*

## Strategy

**P — Person, Problem, Pain**
She is competent, organised and quietly depleted. The surface problem is a stiff neck and lower back from desk work and no core strength. She notices it getting out of the car. The real problem is a low grade guilt: she is excellent at looking after everyone else and demonstrably bad at looking after herself, and booking a class has become a task she keeps failing to do, which compounds the feeling.

**A — Amplify and Aspiration**
Amplify carefully. Her root fear is that the version of herself she recognises is disappearing under everything she is responsible for. That fear must be implied, never stated bluntly, because naming it directly reads as an accusation and she scrolls past. Aspiration: fifty uninterrupted minutes where nobody can reach her and she does not have to make a single decision.

**S — Story and Solution**
Fifty minute reformer class, capped at ten people, on the high street so there is no travel time. SIGNATURE is the entry point. The solution is not the exercise, it is the removal of decisions.

**T — Transformation and Testimony**
From the woman who is always meaning to, to the woman who goes on Tuesdays and Thursdays. The shift is intention to routine, and that is the whole battle. Testimony must be a named local woman in her late thirties, not an influencer.

**O — Offer**
First Time Pass. Six classes for £60 over two weeks. Framed as a two week reset with a beginning and an end, not as a discount.

**R — Response**
Book the two week reset.

**Biggest objection to defuse:** "I will book it and then not go, and waste the money."

## Copy-ready

> **You have been meaning to do this since March.**
>
> You are on top of everything. The diary, the school run, the work thing that was supposed to be simple. Everything is handled.
>
> Except your back, which has started making itself known when you get out of the car. And your neck, which has not properly loosened since about 2023. And the gym membership you cancelled in February, mostly out of guilt.
>
> It is not that you do not know what to do. It is that booking the class has quietly become one more thing on a list you are already losing.
>
> Here is what we would like to offer instead. Fifty minutes, twice a week, on [STREET]. A room with ten people in it, maximum. An instructor who learns your name in the first class and watches what your body is actually doing. Nobody shouting. No leaderboard. No decisions to make except turning up.
>
> You lie down, and for fifty minutes nobody needs anything from you.
>
> Most people arrive expecting it to be gentle. It is not gentle, it is controlled, which is a different thing and considerably more interesting. And most people find that after the third or fourth class, something in the lower back stops arguing.
>
> **[TESTIMONIAL: named local woman, 35 to 45, on the before and after of her first class]**
>
> **Six classes. £60. Two weeks.**
>
> That is £10 a class, and it is deliberately finite. Six classes across two weeks is enough to stop it being a thing you tried and start it being a thing you do. It is available once, for new clients only.
>
> **Book your two weeks at [STUDIO]**

# 2. The Rebuilding Mother

*28 to 42, six weeks to two years postpartum, or pregnant past twelve weeks.*

## Strategy

**P — Person, Problem, Pain**
She has been signed off by a GP, told vaguely to start gentle exercise, and given no plan whatsoever. Weak core, back pain from feeding and carrying, and no idea what movement is safe. She cannot leave the baby easily. She is also, in many cases, lonely, and everything she does is for someone else.

**A — Amplify and Aspiration**
Her root fear is that this is permanent, and that she has already missed the window. The practical amplifier is real and time bound: maternity leave ends, the daytime window closes, and the baby starts crawling. Aspiration: a class she can physically get to, with the baby, where somebody qualified tells her exactly what is safe.

**S — Story and Solution**
The Mama Method. Pre and postnatal safe, pre-crawling babies welcome, designed for mums by mums. Authority outranks warmth for this segment, so instructor qualification must appear early and be specific.

**T — Transformation and Testimony**
From a body that happened to her to a body she is rebuilding on purpose, and from isolated to part of a group. Testimony must state the mother's stage explicitly, because she is calibrating against someone at her exact point.

**O — Offer**
A first Mama Method class free, or a downloadable guide to safe postnatal movement. Both work as reciprocity and both give her a low risk way to assess safety before spending.

**R — Response**
Book your first Mama Method class.

**Biggest objection to defuse:** "What if the baby cries and I ruin it for everyone." Answer it in the ad copy itself. It is the single reason she does not book.

## Copy-ready

> **Your six week check said gentle exercise. This is what they meant.**
>
> Somebody signed you off, told you to start moving again, and then handed you nothing. No plan, no explanation of what is safe, no idea whether the thing you can feel in your middle is normal or something to worry about.
>
> So you have been careful. Which is sensible, and also means that months on, carrying the car seat still goes through your lower back, and the body you are living in still does not feel like it is yours.
>
> **The Mama Method is built for the body you have right now, not the one you had before.**
>
> It is a pre and postnatal safe reformer class, taught by instructors qualified specifically for this stage. Gentle strength building, mobility, and core connection, in that order, at the pace your body is actually ready for.
>
> Bring the baby. Genuinely bring the baby, as long as they are pre-crawling. There is no childcare to arrange and no window to find. And if they cry, nobody in the room will so much as look up, because every other person there has a baby beside them too.
>
> That last part matters more than we expected it to. Most people come for the core work. A good number stay because it is the only hour in the week they are surrounded by adults in exactly the same position.
>
> **[TESTIMONIAL: local mother, stage stated explicitly, e.g. "four months postpartum, first baby"]**
>
> **Your first Mama Method class is on us.**
>
> Come once, see the room, meet the instructor, ask every question you have got. Then decide.
>
> **Book your first class at [STUDIO]**

**Compliance note:** references to pelvic floor and abdominal separation have been deliberately kept general. Do not add specific rehabilitation or recovery outcome claims without substantiation and sign off. Retain the studio's own position that clients past twelve weeks pregnant consult their doctor before attending.

# 3. The Strength Convert

*25 to 40, genuinely fit, arrived because the training started hurting.*

## Strategy

**P — Person, Problem, Pain**
She runs, she lifts, she has done F45 or bootcamps. She is not intimidated by exercise and she is not looking for gentle. Recurring niggles, tight hips, a knee or lower back that flares, a plateau where nothing is improving. She is doing everything right and it is not working, which is not a story she has a framework for.

**A — Amplify and Aspiration**
Her root fear is a real injury that takes away the thing that defines her, and the suspicion that her twenties approach to training has an expiry date she is approaching. Loss aversion framing is strong here. Aspiration: to keep doing the sport she loves for another twenty years without breaking.

**S — Story and Solution**
STRENGTH and SWEAT. Time under tension, jump board intervals, unilateral loading, progressive spring resistance. She responds to mechanism. Explain how it works and she will believe it works.

**T — Transformation and Testimony**
From fit to durable. From someone who trains hard to someone who trains intelligently. Testimony must come from someone recognisably athletic, ideally a runner talking about posture or injury.

**O — Offer**
First Time Pass, framed as a test of whether the class is hard enough for her rather than as a discount.

**R — Response**
Book the SWEAT class and see.

**Biggest objection to defuse:** "Pilates is not a real workout." The only answer is footage. Understating difficulty loses her permanently.

## Copy-ready

> **It is low impact. It is not easy.**
>
> You are fit. That is not in question. You run, you lift, you turn up when you said you would.
>
> And yet the hip is still tight, the left knee still complains on descents, and the lower back has started doing a thing on longer runs that you are choosing not to think about too hard. You have added mobility work. It has not fixed it.
>
> Here is the uncomfortable version. High volume, high impact training builds you strong in the patterns you repeat and leaves everything else behind. The single leg stability, the deep core control, the unilateral work, the small stabilisers around the hip and shoulder. That is the accessory work everybody knows they should do and nobody does.
>
> **That is the entire content of a STRENGTH class.**
>
> Time under tension, controlled and slow enough that you cannot cheat it with momentum. Progressive spring resistance so the load actually increases. Unilateral loading so the strong side stops covering for the weak one.
>
> Or SWEAT, if you want your heart rate involved. Jump board intervals on the carriage, floor work in between, and the specific indignity of finding out that forty five seconds is a long time.
>
> Ten people, maximum. Fifty minutes. Reserve your own bed and plan your week around it.
>
> Most people who train seriously come in slightly sceptical. Most of them are shaking by the fifteenth minute. We would rather you found that out than took our word for it.
>
> **[TESTIMONIAL: recognisably athletic client, ideally a runner, on injury or posture]**
>
> **Six classes. £60. Two weeks.**
>
> Enough to see whether it is hard enough for you. New clients only, once per person.
>
> **Book at [STUDIO]**

# 4. The Midlife Fortifier

*45 to 65, perimenopausal or post menopausal. The largest unclaimed opportunity in the report.*

## Strategy

**P — Person, Problem, Pain**
Her body has changed in ways nobody warned her about. Weight redistributing, sleep fragmenting, joints stiffening, strength quietly leaving. She has read enough to know that muscle mass and bone matter. She resists any framing that categorises her as elderly or in decline, and she will punish a brand that does it.

**A — Amplify and Aspiration**
Her root fear is loss of independence. Not death, not appearance. A fall, a fracture, and needing help. This is the deepest and most actionable fear in the report, and it is usually visible in her own parents. Prevention framing genuinely outperforms gain framing here, which is the reverse of the usual advice. Aspiration: to still be strong, upright and independent in twenty years, on her own terms.

**S — Story and Solution**
Progressive spring resistance work that builds strength and balance without impact, in a small class where the instructor knows what her body needs. The critical reframe is construction, not repair. She has been told her whole adult life that this decade is about managing decline. Telling her it is the most productive decade she has to build strength is the strongest move available.

**T — Transformation and Testimony**
Testimony from women of the same age, stating what they can now do that they could not before. A single testimonial from a 56 year old local woman will outperform ten from thirty year olds, and no competitor in either catchment is doing this visibly.

**O — Offer**
First Time Pass, with daytime class availability made explicit. She has time other segments do not, and it is the capacity every boutique studio struggles to fill.

**R — Response**
Book two weeks and see what your body does.

**Biggest objection to defuse:** "It will be full of twenty five year olds and I will feel out of place." Answer with visible age diversity in every asset.

## Copy-ready

> **Nobody warned you. We will.**
>
> Somewhere in the last few years, the rules changed without notice. The same food, the same habits, a different result. Sleep that used to be automatic. Joints that have opinions in the morning. Strength that has not gone anywhere dramatic, just quietly downwards.
>
> And underneath it, the thing you do not say out loud, which is that you have watched this happen to someone in your own family and you know roughly how the story goes.
>
> Here is the part that does not get said often enough. **This decade is not the one where you manage the decline. It is the most productive decade you have left for building.**
>
> Muscle responds to progressive resistance at sixty. Balance is trainable. Strength is trainable. The window is open, it is just narrower than it was, which is an argument for starting now rather than an argument for not bothering.
>
> Reformer Pilates does this without the joint load of free weights or the impact of running. Spring resistance you can increase over time. Controlled, unilateral work that trains balance and stability rather than just power. Fifty minutes, ten people maximum, an instructor who watches what your body is doing rather than counting reps at the front.
>
> Our daytime classes are quieter and considerably more civilised than the 6pm rush, and a good number of our regulars would not train at any other time.
>
> **[TESTIMONIAL: local woman 50+, stating a specific capability she has now and did not have before]**
>
> **Six classes. £60. Two weeks.**
>
> Long enough to feel the difference in how you get out of a chair. New clients only.
>
> **Book at [STUDIO]**

**Compliance note:** the copy above deliberately avoids specific bone density, osteoporosis and menopause symptom claims and stays with mechanism and capability language. Any hardening of these claims requires substantiation and compliance review before going live.

# 5. The Founding Member

*Oxted only. 35 to 58. She does not want a discount. She wants to have been there first.*

## Strategy

**P — Person, Problem, Pain**
This is the segment where PASTOR has to bend. She has no acute pain. She very probably already attends one of the three or four reformer studios within walking distance of Oxted station and she is not unhappy there. Her surface problem is mild: oversubscribed classes, a timetable that does not quite work, or simple boredom. Her root fear is being ordinary. Being one of the crowd who found out when everybody else found out.

**A — Amplify and Aspiration**
The amplifier is not consequence, it is closure. Ten memberships, permanently, and the status cannot be bought later at any price. Aspiration: to be embedded from day one in what she expects will become the best studio in the town.

**S — Story and Solution**
The credibility bridge is Banstead. An established studio with a 5.0 rating from approximately 185 clients, opening its second site. Without that proof this reads as a new business asking for £2,000 up front, which is a much weaker proposition. Lead every Oxted asset with the Banstead track record, not with the promise of Oxted.

**T — Transformation and Testimony**
From member to founder. Permanent status, unbuyable afterwards. Testimony comes from Banstead clients, and once the first founding members sign, from them.

**O — Offer**
Ten founding memberships. £2,000. 200 credits over twelve months across Reformer, Mat, Barre and Yoga.

**R — Response**
Take one of the ten.

**The single most important instruction in this document:** do not lead on £10 a class. The moment this becomes an ad about value, it stops attracting founders and starts attracting bargain hunters, who will not spend £2,000. Provide the per class figure as a rationalisation she can use afterwards, then move past it quickly. Leading with it also anchors the entire Oxted market at £10.

**Biggest objection to defuse:** "What if it does not open, or opens late, or is not very good." Publish an opening window, a refund or transfer position, and the Banstead record. Without all three the campaign underperforms regardless of budget.

## Copy-ready

> **Ten founding memberships. That is the entire allocation.**
>
> Studio Glide is opening in Oxted.
>
> You already know the town has options. You are probably in one of them, and it is probably fine. This is not an argument about which studio is better.
>
> This is about ten places.
>
> Before we open, we are offering ten founding memberships. Not ten this month. Ten, total, permanently. When they are gone there is no second release, no waiting list that converts, and no way to become a founding member afterwards at any price. That is rather the point of the word.
>
> **What sits behind this is not a new business.**
>
> Studio Glide has been running on Banstead High Street and is rated 5.0 by around 185 clients. Not a handful of reviews from friends. One hundred and eighty five people, and not one of them has anything bad to say about it. In this category that is genuinely rare, and it is the reason we are able to sell out an Oxted studio before the doors open rather than after.
>
> Oxted is the second site, built on everything the first one taught us.
>
> **The membership**
>
> 200 class credits, valid for twelve months. Usable across Reformer, Mat, Barre and Yoga. £2,000.
>
> Founding members get first pick of the opening timetable, and founding status from the day we open onwards.
>
> **[TESTIMONIAL: Banstead client, ideally on why they have stayed]**
>
> **[X] of 10 remaining.**
>
> **Claim a founding membership**

**Required before this runs:** confirmed opening window, published address, and a stated refund or transfer position if opening is delayed. She is being asked for £2,000 by a studio with no published location and no date.

# 6. Composite PASTOR: Oxted Priority List Page

*For the no-offer list capture page. Must work across segments 1 to 4 in the Oxted catchment.*

## Strategy

A composite has to find the one thing that is true across four different women, or it becomes generic. The common thread across every segment in the report is not fitness and it is not price. It is this: **each of them is managing something in their body that they did not choose and have not addressed, and each of them has a reason to have delayed.**

The composite therefore does not attempt to name a specific pain. It names the delay, and offers the smallest possible next step, which is the only thing this page is asking for.

**P** — You have been thinking about reformer Pilates. You have not booked.
**A** — Oxted's studios fill quickly, and the opening timetable will go first to the people who are on the list.
**S** — Studio Glide, proven in Banstead, opening in Oxted.
**T** — 185 five star reviews and a room where beginners are the norm.
**O** — Not a price. Early access booking, seventy two hours ahead of public release, plus opening news first.
**R** — Join the priority list.

## Copy-ready

> **Reformer Pilates is coming to Oxted.**
>
> You have probably been meaning to try it. Most people around here have. It comes up in conversation, it comes up on your phone, and it has been sitting somewhere near the top of the list of things you will get round to.
>
> Studio Glide is opening on [STREET].
>
> We have spent the last few years building a studio on Banstead High Street that is now rated 5.0 by around 185 people. Fifty minute classes, ten people maximum, instructors who learn your name in the first session and watch what your body is actually doing rather than counting from the front. Beginners are not tolerated, they are the norm.
>
> Oxted is next.
>
> **Get on the priority list and three things happen.**
>
> You get the opening week timetable and booking link seventy two hours before anyone else, which in a town this size is the difference between the class you wanted and the class that was left.
>
> You hear about anything we do for the opening before it goes public.
>
> And you find out the date, the address and who is teaching, the moment each is confirmed.
>
> That is it. No commitment, no card details, and we will only email you about Oxted.
>
> **Join the priority list**

# 7. Sequencing Note

The five PASTORs are not interchangeable and should not be softened into each other. The report is explicit that Strength Convert creative will fail if it is a gentler version of Reset Seeker creative, and that Midlife Fortifier creative will fail if it codes as senior fitness.

Suggested rollout, matching the report's phasing:

| Phase | PASTOR builds live | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Now | 5 (Founding Member), 6 (Composite list page) | Only element in the plan with a closing window |
| Weeks 1 to 4 | 1 (Reset Seeker), 2 (Rebuilding Mother) | Volume engine plus the highest intent to reach ratio |
| Weeks 5 to 10 | 4 (Midlife Fortifier), 3 (Strength Convert) | Largest unclaimed opportunity, then high value low volume |

**Blocking dependency:** every T section above is currently a placeholder. Ten filmed client testimonials, recruited deliberately across segments (two mothers, two women aged 50 plus, two runners or gym goers, four general), unblocks all six builds at once and is the single highest leverage creative task on the account.
