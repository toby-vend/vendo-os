# Concept Dental — patient voice quote bank (forums)

> **Read first:** none of these people are Concept Dental patients. Every quote comes from a public UK forum post by a member of the public about their own dental experience (mostly Mumsnet, with Gransnet and MoneySavingExpert). Use them for language and insight in ad copy research only. Never present them as Concept Dental testimonials or reviews, and never attribute them to a named person in an ad.

**Method.** Each thread was downloaded and the post text extracted. Every quote below was checked programmatically against that text: the words, spelling, typos and punctuation are as posted. Line breaks and double spaces are collapsed to single spaces. `…` marks a trim made here. Where a `…` sits inside a quote with no space before it, the poster typed it. Profanity is left as written. Compiled 19 October 2026.

**Tags:** accommodation (how they live around the problem) · failed solution · nearly didn't buy · own words · decision moment · after.

**Caveats.** Mumsnet and Gransnet skew female and the threads are mostly 2019–2026, so most voices for the male personas (Mark, Peter, Barry) are women describing the same situation. Gender-neutral language carries over well, but check tone before putting it in a man’s mouth. NHS fees quoted in threads are out of date. Reddit was not used.

**Patterns — Mark**

- “Plucked up the courage” and “bite the bullet” are the standard phrases for the moment of booking; the decision is framed as courage, not convenience.
- Shame outranks fear: “embarrassed”, “ashamed”, “disgusting”, “tell me off”, “humiliate”, “scolded”. The worry is the dentist’s reaction, not the pain.
- Time makes it worse: “the longer I didn’t go the harder it was”, “put it off for years”, “buried my head in the sand”.
- The after is nearly always relief that it was “nowhere near as bad (or as expensive) as I thought”, plus regret at not going sooner.
- Visible accommodation is about smiling: no smiling or laughing with teeth showing, avoiding photos.

- Converts often say private was “not as bad as you’d think” or “surprised how reasonable”; monthly plans (Denplan, £15–£25 a month) come up again and again as the bridge.

- Implant owners use near-identical language afterwards: “best money I ever spent”, “worth every penny”, “zero regrets”. Regret from those who didn’t centres on bone loss: “too late”.
