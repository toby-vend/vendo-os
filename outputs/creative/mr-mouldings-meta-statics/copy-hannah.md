## H3 | One wall first
**Awareness:** solution aware. **Sophistication:** 3 (mechanism). **Angle:** proper profiled panel moulding, not flat strips.
Research: "The ones that aren't even panelled, and just have slats or beading glued to the wall, are even worse than actual panelling." (Mumsnet, WatchImWatching); "my main goal is to create texture on otherwise boring walls" (Mumsnet, validUsernameHere); Audience Report: the bedroom is the entry room.
Concept name: **Hannah Panel Moulding | One wall first**
Talent: Room photo

**On image:** Start with one wall. / Panel moulding has a real profile, so the boxes cast a shadow line instead of looking stuck on. / Ogee, astragal, ovolo and more / Shop panel moulds

**Headline:** Panelling with a proper profile.

**Primary text:**
Start with one wall. The bedroom behind the bed, or the hallway everyone walks through.

The panelling that looks stuck on is usually flat strips glued to plaster. Paint it and it still looks flat, because there's nothing for the light to catch.

Panel moulding is different. It has a real profile, an ogee, an astragal, an ovolo, so every box casts a shadow line. That's the difference between a feature wall and a craft project.

You mark out the boxes, cut the corners, fix with adhesive and pins, then paint the wall and the moulding the same colour.

We make panel moulds in over a dozen profiles, alongside the matching dado rail and skirting, in our Epsom workshop.

Shop panel moulds and plan your first wall.

One wall in, and you'll know what to do with the rest of the house.

## H4 | Not a builder job
**Awareness:** solution aware. **Sophistication:** 4. **Angle:** answer "I can't do this myself" honestly.
Research: "by Christ if I had known how difficult it was to saw two boards so that the two corners joined up properly at right angles I would never have bothered." (Pistonheads, Ayahuasca); "I live alone and am not very good at DIY." (Mumsnet); creative plan: "Assumes changing it is a builder job."
Concept name: **Hannah Taller Skirting | Not a builder job**
Talent: Room photo

**On image:** "I'd never get the corners right." / Grab adhesive on masonry walls. Adhesive and pins on stud walls. The corners take practice, so we tell you to order 10% extra. / Shop skirting

**Headline:** Mostly glue, filler and paint.

**Primary text:**
"I'd never get the corners right."

That's what stops most people. So here's the honest version of fitting new skirting.

On a flat masonry wall it goes on with grab adhesive. On a stud wall, adhesive plus pins into the studs. Then you fill the joins, caulk the top edge and paint.

The corners are the bit that takes practice. That's why we tell everyone to add about 10% for mitres and mis-cuts, so a wrong cut costs you a length rather than the job.

Primed boards need a light sand, filling and two topcoats. Most people prime and give one coat before fitting, then finish once it's on the wall.

The fix is to match the age of the house. Chamfered, bullnose and square edge profiles all read as modern. Clean lines, no curls, just at a better height than the builder fitted.

A taller modern skirting, an architrave that matches, and maybe one panelled wall in a simple square profile. It looks like it was always meant to be there.
