## 2. Post-purchase survey (Vendo summary, 370 responses, Jul 2025 to Jun 2026)

Source: [MR_Mouldings_Survey_Summary.docx](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Sp-Fu4WfoLNlumcM3fnoBaryp0t3leYK/view)

- **Why customers choose MR Mouldings (127 free-text answers):** exact product match (profile / size, matching existing) 68%; range and selection 64%; price / value 16%; quality 14%; used before 8%; reviews 7%.
- Customer phrasing summarised in the report: "you had the exact profile I needed to match my existing / period mouldings."
- **Who buys (137 answered):** DIY / home renovator 58% (mean order £256); trade professional 28% (£471); property developer ~5% (£2,041); interior designer ~3% (£385).
- **NPS +52** (n=127). 93% found what they were looking for.
- **The one recurring complaint is delivery cost:** "Excessive postage cost", "Delivery charge was excessive", "Delivery cost is crazy", "£42.10 shipping, almost as much as my order", "more delivery options please". Secondary: a delivery slip from 3–5 to 5–7 working days.
- Requested: purchasable samples.
- Quoted in the summary: "Beautiful profiles that are worth paying extra for. I work in the trade and this is as good as I have found, value for money."
- 93 customers consented to being quoted. **The individual consents sit in the survey export, not here; no named survey quote goes on an ad until the specific person is checked against that list.**

## 4. Personas (agreed by the creative team)

Source: [MR Mouldings Strategy Plan](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Tmdq5hXoiGvCxKZXdaKwmFDIlanutd_sSW5FaYuxZDM/edit) and [Paid Creative Strategy deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1PeTZNDH-mdmoyfBCXV5x79_oL2W9f4WqZN5LAtLtPxw/edit), 22–25 Sep 2026. Supporting segment detail from the [Audience Report](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1OEKGknkWZavzTnDIlQvdR7Kv6zn31qQgWe4jFkEVG1E/edit) (Aug 2026).

| Persona | Side | Lead message (creative plan) | What stops them (creative plan) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sophie, period restorer | D2C | Your house already has the profile | Believes a match is only possible in solid timber; worries MDF is a compromise in a period house |
| Hannah, new-build upgrader | D2C | Taller skirting changes the whole room | Doesn't realise the skirting is making the room feel cheap; assumes changing it is a builder job |
| Priya, interior designer | B2B | Matched from a single sample | A near-enough match ruins a scheme and she carries the blame; assumes bespoke means a high price and a complicated process |
| Lee, small renovation firm | B2B | One order covers the whole house | Can't quote until he knows what the package covers and costs; splitting orders across two suppliers |
| Ray, commercial fit-out | B2B | Fire rated stock with the paperwork behind it | Needs documentation he can hand to Building Control; won't specify what he can't evidence |

Audience Report points used in the copy:
- Period owners have often already failed: "taken an offcut to a merchant in a carrier bag and been told it cannot be matched, or been quoted a sum by a joiner that stalled the project." Moment of receptivity: an extension or knock-through where new mouldings meet old; "a merchant or joiner has already said it cannot be matched." Tone: respectful, expert, unhurried; no urgency or discounting.
- New-build owners: 2.4m ceilings, flat plasterboard, "the 70mm the developer specified across the entire site" (the creative plan says "builder-grade 95mm"; copy avoids quoting either number as fact about the viewer's house). Before-and-after is the System 1 trigger; gain framing beats loss framing.
- Meta personal attributes: don't assert something about the viewer ("your house looks cheap"). Frame as a shared observation or third person.
- Seasonality: September to February is the natural high season for interior joinery.

## 6. Conflicts and open checks (carried into every copy file's Notes)

1. **Delivery time:** site says 5–7 working days; Adam said on 25 Sep that stock orders ship in 2–3 days. Ads say nothing faster than the site.
2. **Made-to-match payment order:** the bespoke matching page says pay first, then send the sample; the homepage says "Send us a photo. We'll match the profile." and the FAQ says photo with tape measure works. Ads use "send a sample, drawing or photo" and "quote first" only; no claim about payment timing.
3. **Moisture-resistant MDF:** the FAQ says all MDF mouldings are moisture-resistant grade; the August Audience Report flagged this needs confirming. Not used in ads until Adam confirms.
4. **Fire rated documentation:** Euroclass B is stated on product pages. The FAQ only promises FSC/PEFC certification documents on request. Ray's ads must not promise fire test certificates or Declarations of Performance until Adam confirms what he can send.
5. **Reviews:** the on-site "4.6 from 11,700+" is ~90% templated (generated) text. Not used anywhere. Trustpilot is 4.2 from 128 (13% one-star). Proof in ads is facts only. Needs raising with Adam: DMCC Act 2024 fake-review rules.
8. **Primer finish:** seven genuine reviews complain primed boards arrive rough and need heavy sanding. No ad claims primed boards are "ready to paint".
9. **Returns:** FAQ says no restocking fee on stock returns; a July 2026 Trustpilot review says a 50% restocking fee was deducted. No ad mentions returns.
10. **Delivery cost** is the top genuine complaint (£30 to £70 cited). No ad mentions delivery cost.
6. **Address postcode:** 2024 guidelines say KT19 9BB; site says KT19 9QG. Not used in ads.
7. **No samples, no free-delivery threshold, no live offer:** no urgency lines and no "free delivery" in any ad.
