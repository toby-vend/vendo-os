## Persona choice

Dental Persona Bank (Drive), 01 General Dentistry. **Key persona: Mark, the lapsed patient.** 35 to 55, last visit 5+ years ago. Stopped by the thought of being judged, assumes the bill grows with the gap, doesn't know what happens at a first appointment.

Why Mark over the minis:
- Denise (no NHS place): Stoke Bishop is private and the site makes no NHS claim, so the "no NHS place" angle has nothing to stand on.
- Ray (in pain today): the client stopped the Google Emergency campaign on 13 Oct. There are no same-day slots and the books are full.
- Sophie (family register): Jay wants teens for ortho, but that's a separate campaign idea for later (13 Oct call).
- Mark matches the practice's own nervous-patient page word for word ("six months or six years"). It also suits the lead-quality problem: stating the £85 fee up front filters out people who won't book.

Casting: the client's shoot has no adult male patient. The patient model in the shoot (a woman in her thirties, shown in the chair with the team) is used across all three concepts so the set reads as one person. The copy is written to "you", not "he".

Patterns:
- The real barrier is the telling-off, not the drill. People expect a lecture about the gap.
- The gap feeds itself: the longer it's been, the harder it is to book.
- People assume a long gap means a big bill. A fixed, stated first-visit fee answers that.
- What helped: saying "I'm nervous" up front, and a dentist who explains rather than scolds. Stoke Bishop's nervous-patient page already offers both (extra time, plain language, nothing without consent, a meet-and-greet).

## Awareness and sophistication

- Market: problem aware (they know they need to go) and solution aware (they know what a check-up is). "Cheap check-up" offers are everywhere (sophistication 2–3), and the client's previous agency found discount offers brought "tire kickers" (onboarding call).
- So the ads write at stage 4–5: a mechanism (how the first visit runs, what stops the telling-off) and identity (the person who finally booked it). They don't compete on discount. The price is stated plainly as a filter, not as a deal.
