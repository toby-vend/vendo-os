Headline: the case for further creative investment is holding up on both measures this week.

Week 36 Summary

Meta — UK (31 Aug to 10 Sept)

- ROAS 495% against 371% the same period last year, up 33%. September planned ROAS is 5.0, so the account is on target.
- Cost per purchase £13.79 against £17.76 last year, down 22%. 166 purchases on £2,288 spend.
- Click to purchase rate 2.12% against 1.49%, up 42%. Cost per thousand impressions 28.20 Kr. against 38.56, down 27%.
- Spend running at £208 a day against the £250 daily budget, 83%. The shortfall sits in awareness and retargeting, not the sales campaigns, and widening the retargeting pool this week absorbs most of it.
- Seasonal Best Sellers returned 1,608% on £240. Hi-Vis 501%, ProTex1 473%, both into their strongest season.
- Retargeting is the weak layer at 366% against 563% last year. It is serving the same pool 6.25 times over and reaching only 11,987 people. That is a pool size problem, not creative, and we are widening it this week.

Creative investment case

- Reach: impressions per day up 25% and people reached per day up 30% on last year, at 27% lower cost per thousand. We are not holding ROAS flat while buying that reach, we are improving it, 495% against 371%.
- Lower funnel conversion: the category layer, which is Hi-Vis, ProTex1, Trousers and Seasonal Best Sellers, converted at 2.66% against 1.61% last year, up 65%. Cost per purchase £11.12 against £21.82, down 49%. ROAS 695% against 346%.
- Worth saying plainly: that is two things moving together on one week, not proof of cause. The awareness layer has only been at scale since late August. The scoreboard runs weekly so we can see whether it holds through September and October before committing further.

Shopify view (Week 36, against the same week last year)

- Gross sales £17,920, up 21%. Total sales £20,545, up 27%. Net sales £15,890, up 26%.
- Orders 313, up 32%. AOV £53.35, down 9%. Returning customer rate 31.35%, up 10%.
- Discounts £1,076, down 80% on last year. Last year this week ran clearance and this year it did not, and revenue is still up 21%. That is a better week than the top line alone suggests.
- Sessions 12,286, up 3%. Conversion rate 1.56%, down 15%.

Where the week was lost

- The conversion rate drop is not traffic quality and it is not creative. Adds to cart were 674, up 22% on last year. Reached checkout 319, down 4%. Completed checkout 192, down 12%.
- The ads are working all the way to the basket and the loss is entirely in the last two steps. Shipping charges collected are up 44% year on year, which is the first place we would look, alongside delivery thresholds, payment methods and anything that has changed in the checkout since last year.

Awareness contribution

- £227, 10% of the budget, reached 199,116 people at £1.14 per thousand and carried 46% of everything the account served.
- Over the same period Facebook referred sessions are up 12% and Facebook attributed sales up 23%. Direct and untracked revenue combined moved from £6.5k to £9.7k, up 49%. Meta only ever credits the last click, so that direct movement is the part the platform will never show you.

Key Actions

- Widen the retargeting pool. Frequency 6.25 on 11,987 people is the clearest inefficiency in the account.
- Checkout completion. Adds to cart up 22%, completion down 12%. Worth checking shipping charges and delivery thresholds against last year.
- Hold ProTex1 and Hi-Vis at current scale through September to November, their strongest window.
- Push Seasonal Best Sellers. 1,608% on £240 is the highest return in the account and the budget does not reflect it.
- September offer slot still open. Last year clearance covered this window and this year we have matched the revenue without it, so the decision is whether to add to a good month rather than rescue a weak one.
