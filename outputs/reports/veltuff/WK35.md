UGC view

Picking up your question on UGC value. As agreed when we set it live, UGC's job is top of funnel: it introduces the brand, people go in and have a look, and the purchase usually closes later on a catalogue or retargeting ad, which takes the credit in Meta's reporting. That is why it has not shown up in the summaries so far, they only showed last-click. From this week it reports here on the measures that match the job.

- The funnel data backs the top of funnel role: people who click a UGC ad add to cart at the same rate as every other ad in the account (9% click to add to cart across Jul-Aug). The drop-off only comes at the final purchase step, which lands on the closing ad, and that closing layer ran at 372% over the same period.
- Attention is where it wins: hook rate roughly 3x the account average, the strongest in the account.
- Even judged purely on last-click, it has covered its own cost: 4,255 DKK spend since launch at 236% attributed. The top of funnel contribution sits on top of that floor.
- The genuine issue is delivery, not performance. £16 of spend this week, because Meta optimises to last-click and starves top of funnel creative inside a sales campaign. The ring-fenced UGC budget goes live this week to fix that.
- On the little it did serve this week: 25 landed visitors, 12 adds to cart, 2.46% CTR vs the 1.81% sales-campaign average.
- What we track here weekly as spend scales: cost per visitor landed, adds to cart, hook rate, new reach, and whether the blended prospecting plus retargeting return holds or improves as UGC delivery grows. That last one is the proof the introduction is feeding the close.
