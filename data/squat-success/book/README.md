## Bobby's story (Ch 1, with callbacks throughout)

- Son of an immigrant father; British-Asian. Recurring motif: **tea and cake rusk** with Dad at every big decision.
- Wayward at school ("least likely to succeed", nearly expelled twice). Chose dentistry over medicine because dentists seemed to have the better lifestyle.
- **Rejected from dental school four times**; got in at **Birmingham University** after a heartfelt letter to the Admissions Tutor. Hated dental school; he and his best friend fought for last place and are now among the most successful from their year.
- Thrived for **seven years** at a practice under an exceptional principal (named in acknowledgements as **Resh Diu**, "my first principal"). The principal sold to a corporate; culture collapsed (a colleague moved Bobby's implant consultations into his own diary "to hit targets"; patients as "units of revenue"). Bobby resigned and left dentistry for family property development.
- Dad asked what to do with a family property in **Leamington Spa**; Bobby suggested a dental practice. His former mentor (who had since opened his own squat) said: "Bobby, why haven't you done this sooner?"
- **The build was a disaster:** builders charged VAT while not VAT-registered (fraud); half-built walls with no roof; a neighbour reported them to Health and Safety and the site was shut; the structural engineer (friends with the fired builders) refused to release drawings; Dad hospitalised three times; the night before the CQC inspection his mother rang at 11 PM saying Dad might not survive the night. Bobby nearly quit twice; his wife: "You're not giving up. This isn't who you are." Passed CQC "by the skin of our teeth". Still working 3–4 days a week as an associate during the build.
- **Avenue Dental** opened; over £500 in appointment fees by lunchtime on day one. ~100 people interested at opening (mainly from location visibility, banners, QR codes); 40–50 patients in month one; **first-month revenue £12,000**.
- First hire (experienced nurse) demanded four days' work for five days' pay plus full-time holiday; let go at 18 months. Took nearly two years to build the right team. The nurse who replaced her is still there five years on and is now practice manager.
- Opened "less than five years ago" (as at Jan 2026). Only regret: not doing it sooner. "I actually look forward to Mondays again."
- Other personal details used in the book: had placed ~300 implants pre-opening; confident in restorative and aesthetics; forced into his first live video (10 minutes' notice) at the British Orthodontic Conference; nearly bought a £30,000 dental chair until his mentor's question saved him £15,000.

## The three pillars (the book's core framework)

1. **The Certainty Compass** (Ch 6–7) — making the three critical decisions (business model, location, timing) without falling into the three traps:
   - **Prestige Virus** — choosing to impress peers (posh street, £30k chair). Cure: "Am I choosing this because it's the best solution, or because I want to be seen choosing it?"
   - **Quick-Fix Fever** — rushing to escape your current job (Bobby's builders; Sanjay's 5-year lease after one viewing, cost £20k to break). Cure: mandatory **48-hour pause on any decision over £5,000**.
   - **Analysis Paralysis Syndrome** — researching forever (Meera: 47 areas, 23 criteria, 31 visits, 3 years, no decision). Cure: set decision deadlines.
   - **Triple-Check Treatment:** (1) What's the patient benefit? (2) What's the data saying? (3) What's the worst case, and can I handle it?
2. **The A-Team Algorithm** (Ch 8–10) — market opportunities not jobs; experiential interviews that reveal character; define and embed mission, vision and values before the first hire.
3. **The Invisible Waiting List** (Ch 11–13) — build trust and a following before opening so you open with a queue, not an empty diary. "Trust doesn't happen on demand."

"Every practice that thrived had them. Every practice that struggled was missing at least one." The framework is pitched as diagnostic as well as prescriptive.

## Key numbers and rules of thumb

**Money**
- Startup capital: **£80,000–£100,000 liquid, minimum**.
- Monthly running costs from day one: **£12,000–£20,000** (mostly fixed).
- Total cost to open a practice: typically **£150,000–£250,000** (Ch 14).
- Construction overruns: budget **10–15% contingency**.
- Associates take **40–50% of gross** (45% used in examples); starting with an associate from day one is "usually a recipe for financial disaster".
- Occupancy cost target: **8–12% of revenue**; 15–20% means razor-thin margins. Leamington: £3,500/month against projected £27,000 revenue = 12.6%.
- "Revenue is vanity, profit is reality, cash flow is sanity."
- Healthcare business failure rate: "single digits" (per his bank manager) vs roughly 1 in 4 for transport/storage and 1 in 8 for retail.

**Business model revenue maths (to hit £25k/month)**
- High volume: 100+ patients at £150–250. Medium: 50–60 at £400–600 ("the sweet spot for most"). Low: 10–15 at £2,000+.

**Location**
- General practice: 30,000+ population within 3 miles, 20–25% in target age range.
- Leamington analysis: population 51,232; 38% aged 45+; household income 3% above national average; **12 competitors, all NHS-focused**, traditional décor, limited hours, no online booking, 3–4 week new-patient waits. "Data said yes. Ego said no. I chose data."
- Parking matters more than prestige. Visit candidate sites at Tue 10 AM, Thu 5 PM, Sat 9 AM.

**Timing**
- **70% rule**: ready enough, not perfect. Readiness checklist of 19 items across financial, clinical, psychological and practical: below 10 = not ready; 11–13 = close; 14+ = go.
- Clinical readiness: 3–5+ years post-qualification.
- Best opening months: Jan–Mar and Sept–Oct. Avoid December and August.
- Expect a 12–18 month struggle period; willingness to work 50+ hours/week initially.

**Hiring**
- Cost of one bad hire (nurse on £28,000): **£27,500–£32,000** conservatively. Right hire could generate £50,000+ in extra revenue.

**Content**
- **3-5-7 rhythm**: 3 posts a week minimum, rotate 5 content pillars, 7 minutes a day max.
- 30-second video formula: hook (1–3s), one insight (4–25s), soft CTA (26–30s). "5-minute daily mindset": record 5 minutes, post the best 30–60 seconds.
- Start posting 9–12 months before opening. 3 posts × 40 weeks = 120 pieces of content.
- Best times: 7–9am, 12–1pm, 7–9pm. Instagram + Facebook is the sweet spot for a local practice. 5–10 hashtags.
- Kit: smartphone; optional £30 ring light, £15 tripod.

## Frameworks and lists worth knowing by name

- **Self-assessment** (Ch 4): clinical skills, financial fitness test, mindset reality check, support system.
- **Three crisis points** (Ch 4): the Building Crisis, the Marketing Panic, the Team Turnover.
- **Decision Matrix** (Ch 4): 5 essential requirements (must have all), 5 important factors (need 3), 5 red flags (more than 2 = reconsider).
- **Five fears** (Ch 5): Financial Abyss, Impostor Syndrome, Perfectionist's Paralysis ("PhD in Procrastination"), Rejection Phobia, Success Saboteur. References Susan Jeffers' *Feel the Fear and Do It Anyway* and sport psychologist Dr Jim Taylor ("confident preparedness").
- **Confidence Code** (Ch 5): Evidence File, Worst-Case Recovery Plan, Daily Confidence Builder, Mentor Mirror (find a relatable mentor, e.g. same background), Small Courage Muscle.
- **Employee vs entrepreneur thinking** table (Ch 5), e.g. "What if I fail?" → "What if I don't try?"
- **Decision traps** (Ch 6): Sunk Cost Fallacy, Shiny Object Syndrome, Herd Mentality, Expert Worship ("Success leaves clues, not blueprints").
- **Business model three questions** (Ch 7): what can you confidently do now (Confident and Current / Competent but Rusty / Trained but Not Ready), what revenue do you need, what do you enjoy.
- **Three elements of opportunity marketing** (Ch 8): speak to their pain points, paint the transformation, pre-qualify for culture fit. Salary goes in the P.S., not the headline.
- **Experiential interviewing** (Ch 9): 6:55 AM interview (unusual request test), interrupted interview, practical problem role-play, early arrival observation, team member interaction, unexpected scenario, critique request, paid 2–3 hour working interview.
- **Mission / vision / values** (Ch 10): mission = purpose, vision = destination, values = compass. Templates: "We exist to serve [AUDIENCE] by solving [PROBLEM] through [APPROACH]." Keep values to 3–5, behavioural, decision-testable.
- **Three pillars of pre-opening trust** (Ch 11): Location Visibility (what Bobby did), Community Integration — local Facebook groups and local businesses (works better), Personal Content Creation (works best). "People don't trust practices. They trust people."
- **Five content pillars** (Ch 12): Clinical Excellence, Patient Care, Personal Journey, Community Connection, Educational Value. "Document, don't create" (credited to Gary Vaynerchuk). "Edutainment."
- **Gradual camera path** (Ch 13): text only → photos → Stories → first permanent 30s video → regular video, over ~9 weeks.
- **Priority list + soft launch** (Ch 13): landing page collecting name, email, phone, services of interest; email at 8 weeks, 4 weeks, 2 weeks (priority booking opens) before opening; follow up non-bookers in week 2.
- **Managing the queue** (Ch 13): extend hours, hire an associate, manage expectations, prioritise urgent cases.

## The Squat Success pitch (Ch 14)

- Positioned as **structured support** for dentists who have *already decided* to open a squat. Three things: a sequenced approach to the book's work; direct access to Bobby and a cohort of peers; accountability.
- Explicitly *not* done-for-you. Not for people still deciding (do Chapter 4 instead), people who prefer DIY, or people who can't or don't want to invest.
- "There's a programme fee" — described only as a small percentage of a £150k–£250k practice build. **No price is given in the book.** Details at SquatSuccess.co.uk.
- Mentions a "first Squat Success cohort", workshops, and mentoring calls.
- Closing three choices: this isn't for you / DIY with the book / join Squat Success. "Whatever you decide, decide it properly. Then go and do it."
- Tone is deliberately anti-hard-sell: "I work with dentists who are ready, not dentists who feel pressured."

## Who the book says it's for (Ch 2)

**For:** clinically confident dentists with premium-fee skills; £80–100k saved (or a clear path to it); frustrated by corporate dentistry; willing to push through discomfort; resilient; open to learning.

**Not for:** those wanting a "safe" career; unwilling to invest in growth; expecting overnight success; clinicians who want no business responsibility; those who panic at adversity.

## Voice and recurring lines (useful for copy that sounds like Bobby)

Conversational, self-deprecating, British, story-led, with direct second-person challenges. Common devices: tea and cake rusk; "Here's the thing"; "Let me be blunt"; bold reality statements after each fear ("The Reality: ...").

- "I escaped corporate dentistry and all I got was this thriving dental practice and my conscience back!"
- "Today, I only have one regret – not taking the leap and doing my squat sooner."
- "I actually look forward to Mondays again."
- "Fear isn't the issue. Letting fear make your decisions is the issue."
- "Courage isn't the absence of fear. Courage is fear walking forward."
- "You don't need to be interesting. You just need to be interested."
- "Your personal brand isn't about being famous. It's about being familiar."
- "It's like planning a party but not inviting people until after you've put the food out."
- "Stop recruiting. Start marketing."
- "Perfect is the enemy of posted."
- "Not 'Can I do this?' but 'What happens if I don't?'"
