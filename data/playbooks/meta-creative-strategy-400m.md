# Meta Creative Strategy — Vendo Playbook

How Vendo builds, tests and resources Meta creative, adapted from a Blue Sense Digital mastermind talk. Blue Sense is an Australian performance agency managing roughly £315M a year in ad spend (approx. conversion of the figure in the talk's title); the talk takes its ecommerce creative system and applies it to services, info and B2B. Source: Blue Sense Digital, "The Creative Strategy Behind 400M a Year in Ad Spend" (title figure is the agency's total non-GBP spend, not a benchmark) ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aFRMC2gNbLM)).

**Core principle: media buying is no longer the edge; creative is.** Meta reads the creative and finds the audience for it, so the creative is the targeting. Build genuinely different concepts (persona × angle × offer), test them in volume, and judge them first by how much spend Meta gives them, not by in-platform ROAS.

**Currency note:** the speaker's numbers come from large Australian and US accounts and the currency is not stated (in a related talk the same agency says its figures are AUD). Every money figure below is either converted to approximate GBP as if quoted in US currency (about £1 = 1.27, rounded) or restated as a ratio. If the source is AUD, the true GBP figure is lower still. **None of them is a UK or Vendo benchmark**; treat converted figures as orders of magnitude only. Sections and bullets marked **Vendo adaptation** or **Vendo standard** are ours, not the speaker's; where a Vendo standard conflicts with the video, the Vendo standard wins.

## How to apply this at Vendo

- **Dental lead gen (our core vertical, roughly half of revenue).** Private UK dental practices are services, so the video's "test the offer first" rule applies: consultation offers, finance, open days, price-led packages for high-ticket treatments (Invisalign, implants, composite bonding). Persona and angle give breadth once an offer works. These are long-consideration purchases, so cold ads need room to educate.
- **Vendo standard: lead events.** Meta's health-data rules block sending lead events back for private healthcare, so View Content (VC) is the optimisation event and lead proxy on dental website campaigns. This is a compliance constraint, not a learning-phase tactic. Instant-form leads are cheaper than VC leads, so **never compare cost per lead across objectives**. Compare within the same objective only.
- **Vendo standard: attribution.** The video recommends 7-day click + 1-day engaged view. **Vendo builds every campaign on 7-day click only** (no engaged-view, no view-through). Read the video's ROAS/CPA advice through that setting.
- **Ecommerce (the Shopify brands, e.g. Veltuff, Sword Stall, MR Mouldings).** The full framework applies with the ecommerce testing order (persona → offer → angle → format) and a separate existing-customer campaign. **Vendo standard:** judge results on Shopify revenue, not pixel-attributed ROAS, where the two disagree. Veltuff reports in DKK; never mix its figures with GBP accounts.
- **Plant hire (lead gen, B2B-leaning).** Treat as services: offer first (availability, delivery radius, operator included, account terms), then persona (site manager, small builder, homeowner doing a one-off job). Audiences are small and local, so expect the video's small-market frequency ceiling sooner and run fewer live concepts.
- **Media spend is client pass-through.** Clients pay Meta directly, so any "move a share of media budget into production" advice is the client's money and the client's decision.
- **When results stall, look at the start of the process, not the end.** Idea → concept → format → editing. High frequency is a concept problem; the editor is not the fix.

## 1. Distribution: why creative is now the lever

[1:37](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=97)

- **Media buying hasn't been an edge for about three years.** Hacks like duplicating ad sets and playing with bid caps no longer produce meaningful arbitrage. Arbitrage follows the skill gap, and most operators are still weak at creative ([1:58](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=118)).
- **Every ad has a finite lifetime spend capacity** ([2:24](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=144)). Launch → ramp → peak daily spend → fatigue. Ramping faster doesn't change the area under the curve; it just burns the ad out sooner. Holding spend over time needs a steady flow of new ads.
- **You no longer choose the audience** ([2:53](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=173)). You load the creative, Meta reads it and finds the people. Three similar creatives reach the same pool: frequency rises, reach falls, returns decline ([3:19](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=199)). The fix is creatives different enough to each reach their own pool.
- **The same ad loses power after two views** ([3:46](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=226)). Purchase probability drops after two impressions of the identical creative; third to fifth impressions are increasingly ineffective. Rotating a new creative in lifts it again.
- **Frequency diagnostic** ([4:08](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=248)): ad-level frequency on cold campaigns over the last 30 days should be **below 2** ([4:37](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=277)). Above 2 means a creative diversity problem. Exception: small markets can hit a genuine ceiling ([29:38](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1778)).
- **Vendo adaptation:** Vendo's frequency standard (see the Meta Frequency & Creative Fatigue playbook) uses a **14-day window**, not the 30 days quoted here, and treats longer-consideration dental treatments as needing higher frequency above ad level. A tight local radius around a practice behaves like the video's small-market exception, so check reach against the catchment before calling it a diversity problem.

## 2. Your best-ROAS ad is not the one to scale

[4:44](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=284)

- In one ad set with an unaware ad, a problem-aware ad and a product-aware ad ("buy now, 20% off"), the product-aware ad shows the best ROAS, the unaware ad the worst ([5:05](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=305)).
- Meta sequences them: unaware first, problem-aware next, product-aware third, acting as retargeting. The whole purchase is credited to the last ad ([5:31](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=331)).
- **The 6x bottom-of-funnel ad is not the one to scale. The 1.4x unaware ad is why the 6x ad got any purchases** ([5:55](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=355)).
- Four consequences: concept diversity (one concept per ad set), format variety ([6:22](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=382)), volume, and separate top- and bottom-of-funnel KPIs because bottom of funnel is always over-attributed.
- **Vendo adaptation:** the same logic applies to cost per lead in dental. A cheap-CPL "book your free consultation" ad is often harvesting people warmed by the educational or problem-led ads. Don't switch off the higher-CPL cold ads on CPL alone.

## 3. Concept architecture: persona × angle × offer

[6:50](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=410)

- **A concept = persona (who) + angle (how you cut through) + offer (what you sell).** Change any one and you reach a different person ([7:14](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=434)).
- **Worked example: creatine for women 40+.** Angle A menopause, angle B preventing age-related injuries such as falls ([7:46](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=466)). Same person, different problems, different sub-audiences. Swap the offer to collagen powder and the same women with the same problem become a new audience ([8:09](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=489)).
- **A persona is a problem, a desire and a buying trigger, not a job title** ([8:19](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=499)). Not "agency owners", but "an agency owner at about £50K a month in revenue where delivery still runs through them, who hasn't had a clear week off in two years" ([8:41](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=521)). A fleshed-out persona makes scripting much better.
- **AI for personas:** build them by hand first, have AI expand them, expect material that doesn't match real buyers, cut back to the best five ([9:07](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=547)).
- **The angle is the argument** ([9:07](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=547)): problem agitation first, a contrarian truth, a curiosity gap opened early and closed late, and a transformation element. Transformation is the strongest element in services and info, as long as it stays compliant ([9:32](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=572)).
- **Lean towards pain-led content** ([11:30](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=690)). Alo Yoga gets away with desire-led ads because of its huge brand and off-account activity ([11:59](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=719)). Small businesses shouldn't copy the biggest global brands.
- **Vendo adaptation (dental):** personas come from the treatment problem, not the demographic. "Denture wearer whose plate slips when eating out" and "adult who hides their smile in photos" are different personas for different offers (implants vs aligners). Transformation content (before-and-afters, patient stories) must follow GDC and ASA/CAP rules.
- **Vendo adaptation (copy):** this matches Vendo's existing PAS (problem–agitate–solve) rule for statics: amplify the persona's pain deeply before the solution.

## 4. Test the offer first outside ecommerce

[9:42](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=582)

| Business type | Testing priority (most to least important) |
| --- | --- |
| Ecommerce | Persona → Offer → Angle → Format |
| Info / B2B / SaaS / services | **Offer** → Persona → Angle → Format |

- Outside ecommerce you can pivot the product, and so the offer, far more easily ([9:32](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=572)). In ecommerce the offer is the biggest lever but barely movable; you can only adjust mechanics such as bundles and discounts ([10:03](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=603)).
- **Each offer test is its own concept, in its own ad set, with at least 10 assets** ([10:03](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=603)). Persona gives breadth once offer and angle work ([10:35](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=635)).
- **Offer, not acquisition, is often the real bottleneck** ([32:17](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1937)). The speaker's example: a business fixated on cutting its cost per qualified booked call by about 30%, when its real problem was an offer with poor retention and lifetime value ([32:42](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1962)).
- **Vendo adaptation:** for dental, treat the practice's offer (consultation price, finance terms, open-day incentive, package) as the first test variable before tweaking angles. Never promise or invent an offer the practice hasn't confirmed.

## 5. Hooks: the hook earns the second line, not the sale

[12:15](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=735)

- **About 80% of viewers never get past the hook** ([11:59](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=719)). The hook is a promise of relevance that gets them to the second line, not a scroll-stopping trick ([12:25](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=745)).
- **Three layers:** visual, audio, on-screen copy.
- **Test hooks, not bodies** ([12:54](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=774)). Someone who watches halfway will probably finish and convert, so the bottleneck is the first 20 seconds. Record **3–5 hooks per body** ([13:16](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=796)), each one coherent with the body.
- **Hook testing still works post-Andromeda** (Meta's newer delivery system that demands creative diversity) ([15:55](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=955)). The speaker has seen about £80,000 (approx.) of extra spend go through a single ad from a couple of hook swaps ([16:27](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=987)).
- **Avoid persona callouts** such as "Agency owners doing over six figures a month, listen up" ([13:18](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=798)). They don't agitate a problem, and Meta can't target by revenue, so one user sees several callouts and realises none is personal ([13:48](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=828)). Hormozi example: "If you're a dental clinic…", "If you're a personal trainer…", then the one that fits no longer feels personal ([14:18](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=858)).
- **Innovate between red and blue ocean** ([15:02](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=902)): reposition into something new where demand is already proven. Copied messaging lands you in the same crowded space; Blue Sense's own scripts have been copied word for word by competitors ([15:30](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=930)).
- **Vendo adaptation (dental callouts are different):** a location + pain callout such as "Exeter! If your dentures slip…" is not the callout the video warns against. Location is genuinely targetable, so the ad is only served to people in that area and stays personal, and the pain half agitates the problem. What to avoid is callouts on attributes Meta can't target (income, job seniority) or callouts with no pain in them. Confirm the ad's target areas with the practice; they are often different from the practice's own town.

## 6. Formats: concept first, then format

[16:33](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=993)

- **Working backwards from format is a rookie mistake** ([16:27](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=987)). "We're testing UGC" is not a strategy. The script, idea, persona and angle make the ad work.
- **If the concept needs teaching, the ad needs length** ([16:56](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1016)). The colder the audience, the longer the ad; warm audiences can get "here's the product, here's the offer" ([17:22](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1042)).
- **Formats that hold spend in B2B and info** ([17:22](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1042)): long-form video sales letters (the most spend of any ad type the speaker has seen), founder talking-head, screen recordings (Loom-style), advertorial funnels, podcast cut-downs ([17:51](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1071)).
- **Partnership ads are a must** in info and B2B ([17:51](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1071)); roughly 30–40% of spend in the speaker's top accounts (the transcript is garbled here, so treat the figure as approximate).
- **Vendo adaptation:** for dental, the "founder" is the dentist or clinician on camera; educational talking-heads suit treatments people need explaining (implants, aligners, sedation). Cold treatment ads need more length than open-day or offer reminders to warm audiences.
- **Vendo standard (dental creative):** never use AI-generated people in scrubs or fake dentists; only the practice's real clinicians appear as clinicians. Statics lead with lifestyle imagery cast to the persona's age (people at home, with friends, at the mirror), not practice interiors or dentist stills. No cartoon characters or mascots in any Vendo ad; animated ads are motion-graphics explainers (type, icons, UI).

## 7. Testing structure: one concept per ad set, 10+ ads each

[11:10](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=670)

- **Typical hit rate is about 5%, so roughly 20 ads find one winner** ([18:11](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1091)–[18:23](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1103)). Put **10+ ads in each concept**; three per concept is noise. Most abandoned concepts were never properly tested ([18:50](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1130)).
- **Baseline structure** ([18:50](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1130)):
  1. Testing campaign, one concept per ad set, 10+ ads each.
  2. Optional scaling campaign for larger businesses or fast-ramping webinar/event funnels: move **post IDs** into a large-budget CBO to keep social proof ([19:17](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1157)).
  3. Existing-customer campaign: always separate in ecommerce; services and B2B usually don't need one.
- **Launch new ad sets rather than adding ads to a winning one**; adding ads resets its sequencing ([11:30](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=690)).
- **Why one concept per ad set** ([30:29](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1829)–[30:57](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1857)): cleaner data (ROAS gets less reliable further down the structure), no ad-level sequencing confusion, and slightly better delivery since targeting still sits at ad set level.
- **Vendo adaptation:** this matches Vendo's CBO-scaling + ABO-testing builds and the "one concept per test ad set" rule. On small single-practice budgets, run fewer concepts at once (the video's lowest tier is about three live) rather than thinning each below 10 ads.
- **Vendo standard (naming):** ad names follow `<Ad set concept> | Format | Talent | Detail | YYMMDD`, so every ad shows which concept (ad set) it belongs to. One concept per ad set makes this naming meaningful. No "Copy" suffixes and no "-" placeholders for unknown fields.

## 8. Measurement: trust amount spent first

[19:28](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1168)

Metrics in order of reliability:

1. **Amount spent.** Budget distribution is Meta telling you which creative it can use. An ad with double the spend at a lower return is usually the better ad ([19:47](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1187)). Ask why it wins spend: low frequency, good hook/hold, high CTR. Often it's pulling new people in for the mid- and bottom-funnel ads to convert ([20:11](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1211)).
2. **ROAS or CPA.** The video says remove 1-day view and use 7-day click + 1-day engaged view ([20:11](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1211)).
3. **CPC and CTR.**
4. **Hook rate and hold rate.** Diagnostic only: they show which part of an asset to rebuild, but aren't directly correlated with performance. Don't use them as a leading indicator ([20:36](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1236)).

- **Vendo adaptation:** Vendo reads CPA on **7-day click only**. For dental, CPA means cost per lead within one objective (instant-form leads and VC leads are separate benchmarks). For ecommerce, anchor ROAS on Shopify where the pixel is unreliable.

## 9. ABO vs CBO is a risk decision

[20:36](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1236)

| | CBO (Meta allocates across ad sets) | ABO (budget set per ad set) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distribution | About 20% of ads take 80% of spend | Spend forced across more ads |
| Efficiency | Best | Slightly lower |
| Risk | Worst portfolio position: when top ads fatigue, spend shifts to unproven assets and the account swings ([21:02](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1262)) | Much lower: more offers, angles and concepts working at once |

- **Middle ground the agency uses a lot: CBO with minimum spend limits per ad set** ([21:26](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1286)). "It's a risk decision, not a performance one."

## 10. Volume and production

[21:26](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1286)

- **Nobody can reliably pick winners** once ads are near winner quality, which is why hit rates rarely exceed about 20% ([21:56](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1316)). The skill is knowing the quality bar, then maximising volume above it.
- **What matters, in general order:** volume (above the bar) → concept → hook → storytelling → editing → talent. The order varies: a famous face makes talent number one ([22:54](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1374)); for some audiences storytelling comes first. Volume below the bar is junk ([23:20](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1400)).
- **Rule of thumb: about one new ad per month per £800 of monthly spend** (approx. conversion of the source's round 1,000 figure) ([23:20](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1400)). At about £80k a month that's about 100 new ads, building to a couple of hundred live. It is a ratio, so it scales down: the logic matters more than the exact figure.
- **Better method 1, work back from revenue** ([23:48](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1428)): average spend per ad × average return on 7-day click = expected revenue per ad launched; divide the new-customer revenue target by that. Source example, converted approx.: £800 per ad × 3x = £2,400 per ad, so a £240,000 new-customer revenue target needs 100 creatives. Info businesses use cash collected, not revenue.
- **Better method 2, churn analysis:** count winners at the start of the month, how many fatigued by the end, and work back through the hit rate to the volume needed ([24:14](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1454)).
- **Content mix** ([24:35](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1475)): 50% winner replication, 20–30% iterations, 20–30% net new concepts. Not 80% replication: brands have come to Blue Sense near bankruptcy after one ad carried roughly £2.5–4M (approx.) of spend over two years, then fatigued with nothing in the pipeline ([25:08](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1508)).
- **Don't automate ideation** ([25:34](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1534)). AI drifts to the average. Build a scripting skill, but stay closely involved in the inputs; briefing and scripting are the highest-leverage work.
- **Vendo adaptation:** use the ratio as a rough check in £ terms (Vendo rounds it to about one new ad a month per £1,000 of monthly spend). It is a direction-of-travel guide, not a tested benchmark for UK dental. For dental lead gen, run method 1 on leads or booked consultations instead of revenue.

## 11. Production budget by spend level

[26:00](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1560)

| Monthly spend (source tiers, approx. £) | Production | Live concepts | New ads per month | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Under ~£25k | iPhone; your own time | ~3 | As many as you can script well | Learn what good looks like |
| ~£25k–£80k | iPhone + some high production; ~10% of media budget to pre-production, shooting and talent | 5–6 | 30–100 | Concept diversity; a new funnel working |
| ~£80k+ | Diversify creators; % falls with scale | 8–10 | 100+ | A system that runs without you |

- **Common mistake: not putting about 10% of media budget into production** ([26:32](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1592)). "Production" means scripting and ideation, shooting and talent, not fancy cameras. Example: at about £80k a month, move about £8k to a scriptwriter, an ideas person and new talent (e.g. a woman on camera if a man usually fronts) ([27:00](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1620)). The speaker claims about 50% better performance; people avoid it because they see production as a sunk cost ([27:28](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1648)).
- 5–6 live concepts doesn't mean 5–6 winners; you may have 1–2 winners while still putting 20–30% into new tests ([27:53](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1673)).
- At the top tier (~£80k+ a month), build a system so 100 ads still get made when the founder is on holiday ([28:21](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1701)–[29:10](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1750)).
- **Vendo adaptation:** these tiers are from large Australian and US ecommerce accounts. Most Vendo clients sit well below the bottom tier (dental packages cap media at a few thousand pounds a month), so read the bottom row: few live concepts, scripts doing the heavy lifting, the client's shoot day used for volume. When proposing production spend, frame it in £ as a share of the client's Meta budget (the video's own example moves 10% of media into pre-production, [32:42](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1962)), spent on whatever the account is weakest at. Media is client pass-through, so never commit a client to a budget split they haven't agreed.

## 12. Six things to do in the next 30 days

[29:11](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1751)

1. **Audit frequency.** Last 30 days, sort by spend, check cold-campaign ad frequency; above 2 is a concept problem (persona, angle or offer), not editing ([30:04](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1804)–[30:29](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1829)). Vendo reads this on its 14-day standard.
2. **Count new ads per month** against roughly one per £1,000 of monthly spend (Vendo's rounded version of the source ratio).
3. **Move to one concept per ad set.**
4. **Write 20 specific personas by hand** from the last 20 buyers or sales calls: who they are, interests, why they came, problems, which angle resonated. Then enrich with AI ([30:57](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1857)–[31:46](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1906)). Vendo adaptation: for dental, use the practice's consultation notes and treatment-coordinator calls.
5. **Run at least one offer test** ([31:46](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1906)).
6. **Set a production budget as a percentage of ad spend** and spend it on the weakest link: scripting, ideation, production or trying something new ([33:08](https://youtu.be/aFRMC2gNbLM?t=1988)).
