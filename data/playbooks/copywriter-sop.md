# Vendo Copywriter SOP: Profiles, Process and Audit

Sep 26, 2026 · @Toby

## 1. How to use this SOP

This SOP turns the best-documented copywriters and consumer psychologists into one repeatable system for writing, reviewing and auditing paid ad copy at Vendo.

**Who it is for:** anyone writing or reviewing copy for Meta, Google or landing pages (account managers, paid social, creative strategists, freelancers).

**Three modes:**

| Mode | When | Sections to use | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Write | New campaign, new angle, creative refresh | 3, 4, 5 | Brief, angle bank, hooks, primary text, headlines, LP copy |
| Review | Before anything goes live | 3, 6 | Pass / fix list against the QA checklist |
| Audit | Monthly, or when CPA rises / ROAS falls | 7 | Scorecard, diagnosis by funnel stage, action list |

**Golden rule:** research before writing. Ogilvy, Schwartz, Halbert and Bencivenga all agreed the research matters more than the writing. Budget 40 to 60% of copy time for research.

**Labelled assumptions in this SOP:**

- Benchmarks are industry ranges for cold prospecting traffic, not Meta-published figures. Replace them with each account's own 90-day medians once available.
- Dental and e-commerce examples reflect Vendo's main verticals. All example claims (reviews, prices, finance) must be checked against the client's real facts and UK ad rules (ASA / CAP, GDC guidance for dental) before use.

## 2. Profile analysis: who to learn from and what to steal

Each profile gives one job in the funnel. Together they cover research, attention, persuasion, conversion and measurement.

### 2.2 Psychologists and effectiveness researchers

| Name | Work | Core idea | How it changes the copy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Robert Cialdini | *Influence* (1984), *Pre-Suasion* (2016) | Seven levers: reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity | Pick one or two levers per ad deliberately. Unity amplifies the others. |
| Daniel Kahneman | *Thinking, Fast and Slow*; prospect theory | Most choices are fast and intuitive (System 1). Losses feel roughly twice as strong as gains. | Make ads effortless to process. Test loss framing and risk reversal. |
| Byron Sharp | *How Brands Grow* (Ehrenberg-Bass) | Mental availability: the chance a buyer thinks of you when buying. Growth comes from light buyers. Distinctive assets build memory. | Consistent brand codes in every ad. Reach broad category buyers. |
| Les Binet and Peter Field | *The Long and the Short of It* (996 IPA cases) | About 60% brand, 40% activation is optimal for most consumer brands. Emotional campaigns were nearly twice as likely to drive large profit gains. | Keep emotional, broad brand creative in the mix, not only offer ads. Don't force one ad to do both jobs. |
| Rory Sutherland | Ogilvy; behavioural science; *Alchemy* | Small, seemingly illogical reframes can beat rational improvements. | Test reassurance cues and reframes, not just discounts. |

### 2.3 What they all agree on

1. Research beats writing talent.
2. Specific beats clever.
3. The first line decides whether anything else is read.
4. Emotion decides; facts justify.
5. Every claim needs proof.
6. Test, measure, iterate. Opinion is not data.

## 3. The 12 laws of Vendo copy

Every piece of copy must pass all 12. Each law names its source so the reasoning can be checked.

1. **Research first (Ogilvy, Wiebe, Georgi).** No copy is written without a completed research brief (Section 4, Step 1).
2. **One big idea per ad (Albuquerque).** If the ad needs two sentences to say what it's about, it's two ads.
3. **Match the awareness level (Schwartz).** Unaware and problem-aware audiences get the problem first. Product-aware audiences get the offer first.
4. **The hook is the ad (Caples, Hott).** The first line or first 3 seconds must stop the scroll and signal "this is for you". Meta may truncate or recombine everything else, so the hook must work alone.
5. **Speak in the customer's words (Wiebe, Levinger).** At least one phrase per ad should come from real VoC (reviews, comments, calls, forums).
6. **Specific, visual, falsifiable (Ogilvy, Dry).** "Whiter teeth" fails. "Three shades whiter in one 60-minute appointment" passes.
7. **Emotion decides, logic justifies (Binet and Field, Goff, Kahneman).** Lead with the feeling (embarrassment, fear, pride, relief). Follow with the facts that make it OK to act.
8. **Proof for every claim (Bencivenga, Cialdini).** Pair each benefit with evidence: reviews, numbers, credentials, before-and-afters, guarantees.
9. **Reduce the risk (Kahneman, Sutherland).** Name and remove the fear of acting: cost, pain, time, judgement, commitment.
10. **One clear action (Goff, Marshall).** One CTA, one next step, no confusion. Confused people don't buy.
11. **Look native, stay distinctive (Hott, Sharp).** Formats should feel like feed content, but brand codes (colour, logo, voice) must be consistent enough to build memory.
12. **Test, don't guess (Hopkins, Denney).** Every launch has a hypothesis. Every result is logged with why it won or lost.

## 4. The writing process: 8 steps

Follow in order. Steps 1 to 3 are research and strategy; do not skip to Step 4.

### Step 1. Research (40 to 60% of total time)

**Goal:** know the customer better than the client does.

**Sources to mine (minimum three):**

- Client's Google and Trustpilot reviews (5-star for desires, 1 to 3-star for fears and objections)
- Competitor reviews (what people wish they'd had)
- Reddit, Mumsnet, Facebook groups, forums
- Comments on the client's and competitors' ads
- Meta Ad Library: competitor ads running 30+ days (long runtime is a proxy for a winner)
- Sales call notes, enquiry forms, FAQs from the client's front desk
- Account data: past winners and losers with their metrics

**Capture in a VoC bank (copy-ready template):**

| Category | Exact customer phrase | Source | Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pain / problem | "I hide my smile in every photo" | Google review | High |
| Desire / dream outcome |  |  |  |
| Fear / objection |  |  |  |
| Failed alternatives |  |  |  |
| Trigger moment (why now) |  |  |  |
| Decision criteria |  |  |  |

### Step 2. Define the audience and awareness level (Schwartz)

| Awareness level | What they know | Lead with | Example opening (dental) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unaware | Nothing about the problem | A story, identity or pattern interrupt | "Why do some people never smile with teeth in photos?" |
| Problem aware | Feel the pain, don't know solutions | The problem, in their words | "Tired of hiding your smile?" |
| Solution aware | Know solutions exist, not yours | Your mechanism / why yours is different | "Straighter teeth without metal braces" |
| Product aware | Know you, not convinced | Proof and risk reversal | "1,200+ five-star reviews. Free consultation." |
| Most aware | Ready, need a nudge | The offer and deadline | "Free whitening with Invisalign, booked by 31 Oct" |

**Rule of thumb:** cold prospecting is mostly problem or solution aware. Retargeting is product or most aware.

Also rate **market sophistication** (Schwartz): if competitors all make the same claim ("straighter teeth"), don't repeat it bigger. Introduce a new mechanism or a more specific claim.

### Step 3. Build the angle bank and pick the big idea

An angle is the reason this person should care now. Generate 8 to 12, then pick 3 to 5 to test.

**Angle sources:**

- A specific pain (embarrassment, pain, time wasted)
- A specific desire (confidence, wedding photos, new job)
- An objection flipped ("too expensive" becomes "from £X a month")
- A mechanism (Georgi): the named reason it works
- An identity / unity lever (Cialdini): "For Reigate parents", "For nervous patients"
- A trigger moment: new year, wedding season, back to school
- Social proof or authority: "The clinic 400 local families trust"

**Big idea test (Albuquerque):** write the angle as one sentence of 16 words or fewer. If you can't, it isn't clear enough.

**Cialdini lever selection:** assign one primary and one secondary lever per angle.

| Lever | Copy expression | Best stage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reciprocity | Free guide, free consultation, free scan | Cold |
| Commitment and consistency | Quiz, small first step, "Check if you're suitable" | Cold / warm |
| Social proof | Review counts, testimonials, "most popular" | All |
| Authority | Credentials, years, awards, specialist titles | Warm |
| Liking | Real team faces, founder voice, humour | All |
| Scarcity | Limited slots, deadlines (must be true) | Hot |
| Unity | Local identity, shared values, "people like us" | Cold / warm |

### Step 4. Write the hooks (minimum 10 per angle)

The hook is the first line of primary text, the headline on the image, or the first 3 seconds of video.

**Hook types (write at least one of each, then cut to the best 3 to 5):**

1. Call-out: "Nervous about the dentist?"
2. Question in their words: "Do you cover your mouth when you laugh?"
3. Bold specific claim: "Straighter teeth in 6 months, no metal"
4. Counterintuitive: "Whitening toothpaste is making your teeth look worse"
5. Story open: "I didn't smile in photos for 11 years. Then…"
6. Social proof lead: "4.9 stars from 1,200 local patients"
7. Loss frame: "Every month you wait, crowding gets harder to fix"
8. Mechanism: "The 3D scan that shows your new smile before you commit"
9. Offer-first (most aware only): "Free consultation this week only"
10. Pattern interrupt / native: sticky note, notes app, text message screenshot

**Hook quality check:** does it (a) stop a stranger, (b) signal who it's for, (c) make them want the next line?

### Step 5. Write the body

Use one framework per ad (Section 5). Whatever the framework, the body must do four jobs in order:

1. **Agitate or deepen** the feeling from the hook (one or two lines)
2. **Introduce the solution and mechanism** (why this works)
3. **Prove it** (review, number, credential, before-and-after)
4. **Remove the risk** (price, pain, time, commitment)

**Length guide for Meta:**

| Placement | Primary text | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feed, cold | 1 to 3 short sentences, or long-form story test | Hook must work in first 125 characters |
| Reels / video | One hook line (about 25 to 40 words) | Video carries the message |
| Stories | 15 to 20 words maximum | Heavily truncated |
| Retargeting | Short, proof and offer led | They already know you |

### Step 6. Write the CTA

- One action, stated plainly: "Book your free consultation", not "Learn more about our services".
- Add the risk reducer next to it: "No obligation", "Takes 60 seconds", "0% finance available".
- Match the CTA button to the stage (Learn More for cold, Book Now for hot).

### Step 7. Write headlines and descriptions

- Meta headline (under the image): the benefit or offer in 5 to 8 words. It must make sense without the primary text.
- Google RSA: see Section 5.4.

### Step 8. Build variants and the test plan

- **Test one variable at a time:** angle first, then hook, then format, then body.
- **Per angle:** 3 to 5 hooks with the same body, so hook performance is readable.
- **Write the hypothesis before launch:** "Loss-framed hooks will beat gain-framed hooks on hook rate for problem-aware adults 35 to 55."
- **Log results** in the creative tracker with why it won or lost (Denney), not just the numbers.

## 5. Frameworks and copy-ready templates

Pick the framework that matches the awareness level. Fill the brackets from the VoC bank, never from imagination.

### 5.1 Framework selector

| Framework | Structure | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PAS | Problem, Agitate, Solution | Problem-aware cold traffic |
| AIDA | Attention, Interest, Desire, Action | General cold, video scripts |
| BAB | Before, After, Bridge | Transformation offers (smiles, fitness) |
| 4U headline | Useful, Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific | Headlines, Google Ads |
| Story (Halbert) | Character, struggle, discovery, result, invitation | Long-form Meta, advertorials |
| Proof stack | Claim, proof, proof, proof, offer | Product-aware retargeting |
| Mechanism (Georgi) | Problem, why others failed, the mechanism, proof, offer | Sophisticated markets |

### 5.2 Meta primary text templates

**A. PAS (cold, problem aware)**

```markdown
[Hook: the problem in their exact words]

[Agitate: the moment it hurts most, one line]

[Solution + mechanism: what we do and why it works]
[Proof: review count / stat / credential]

[Risk reducer] 👉 [CTA]
```

**B. BAB (transformation)**

```markdown
Before: [life with the problem, specific scene]
After: [life without it, specific scene]
The bridge: [treatment/product + mechanism]

[Proof line]
[CTA + risk reducer]
```

**C. Proof stack (retargeting)**

```markdown
[Offer or headline claim]

⭐ [Review snippet 1]
⭐ [Stat or result]
⭐ [Credential or guarantee]

[Scarcity, only if true] [CTA]
```

**D. Native / ugly ad (Hott)**

```markdown
[Sticky note / notes app / text message format]
[One line in a real person's voice, no brand polish]
[Brand shown clearly but secondary]
```

### 5.3 Video script template (15 to 45 seconds)

| Seconds | Section | Job | Metric it drives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 3 | Hook | Stop the scroll, signal who it's for | Hook rate |
| 3 to 10 | Problem / agitate | Make them feel seen | Hold rate |
| 10 to 25 | Solution + mechanism + proof | Build belief | Hold rate, CTR |
| 25 to 35 | Risk reversal + offer | Remove the reason not to act | CTR |
| 35 to 45 | CTA | One clear action, on screen and spoken | CTR, CVR |

**Rules:** captions always on, brand visible in the first 5 seconds (Sharp), real people over stock, one idea per video.

### 5.4 Google Ads (RSA) template

Perry Marshall's rule: the ad's only job is to earn the right click. Match the keyword, qualify the searcher.

| Slot | Count | What to write | Example (dental implants, Reigate) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Keyword headline | 2 to 3 | Echo the search term | Dental Implants in Reigate |
| Benefit headline | 3 to 4 | Specific outcome | Eat & Smile With Confidence Again |
| Proof headline | 2 to 3 | Numbers, reviews, credentials | 4.9★ From 1,200+ Patients |
| Risk reducer headline | 2 | Finance, free consult, guarantee | Free Implant Consultation |
| CTA headline | 1 to 2 | Action + ease | Book Online In 60 Seconds |
| Descriptions | 4 | Benefit + proof + CTA, 90 characters max | Specialist implant dentist. 0% finance available. Book your free consultation today. |

**Pin** only the keyword headline to position 1 if relevance is weak; otherwise let Google rotate.

### 5.5 Landing page template (post-click)

1. **Headline:** matches the ad's promise word for word (message match).
2. **Subheadline:** the mechanism or the specific outcome.
3. **Hero proof:** star rating, review count, credential, before-and-after.
4. **Problem section:** in VoC language (Wiebe).
5. **Solution and how it works:** 3 steps, simple.
6. **Proof block:** testimonials with names, photos, specifics.
7. **Objection handling / FAQ:** price, pain, time, suitability.
8. **Offer and risk reversal:** what they get, what it costs, what they risk (nothing).
9. **CTA repeated:** at least 3 times, same wording.
10. **Ogilvy check:** give all the information they need. Long pages are fine if every line earns its place.

## 6. Pre-launch copy QA checklist

Nothing goes live until every box is ticked. A reviewer other than the writer signs it off.

**Strategy**

- [ ] Research brief completed with at least three VoC sources
- [ ] Awareness level named, and the opening matches it
- [ ] Big idea fits in 16 words or fewer
- [ ] Primary and secondary Cialdini levers chosen deliberately
- [ ] Hypothesis written down

**Hook**

- [ ] Works on its own if everything else is cut
- [ ] Signals who it's for within the first line / 3 seconds
- [ ] Uses a real customer phrase or a specific number
- [ ] At least 3 hook variants per angle

**Body**

- [ ] Every benefit has proof next to it
- [ ] No vague adjectives ("amazing", "best", "quality", "state of the art") without evidence
- [ ] Passes Harry Dry's tests: visual, falsifiable, only we could say it
- [ ] Main fear or objection addressed (price, pain, time, judgement)
- [ ] Sentences short; reading age about 11 to 12 (Flesch-Kincaid grade 6 to 8)

**Action**

- [ ] One CTA only, with a risk reducer beside it
- [ ] CTA button matches the funnel stage
- [ ] Landing page headline matches the ad's promise

**Brand and format**

- [ ] Brand identifiable within 5 seconds or on first glance (distinctive assets)
- [ ] Format feels native to the placement
- [ ] Captions on all video

**Compliance (non-negotiable)**

- [ ] All claims, prices, review counts and stats verified with the client
- [ ] Scarcity and deadlines are genuinely true
- [ ] ASA / CAP Code: no misleading claims, before-and-afters are genuine and typical
- [ ] Dental: GDC guidance on advertising followed (no guarantees of outcome, correct titles, no "specialist" unless on the GDC specialist list); prescription-only medicines (e.g. Botox) not advertised to the public
- [ ] Meta policy: no "you" statements implying personal attributes ("Are you overweight?"), no sensational health claims
- [ ] Finance wording checked if credit is promoted (FCA rules apply to the provider's representative example)

## 7. Account copy audit

The audit finds which stage of the funnel is broken, then which psychological job the copy is failing. Run it monthly, and whenever CPA rises 20%+ or ROAS falls 20%+ over 14 days.

### 7.1 Set up the columns (Meta Ads Manager)

Hook rate and hold rate are not stock columns. Create custom metrics:

| Metric | Formula | What it measures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook rate (thumbstop) | 3-second video plays ÷ impressions | Did the scroll stop? |
| Hold rate | 15-second plays (or ThruPlays) ÷ 3-second video plays | Did the message land? |
| CTR (link) | Link clicks ÷ impressions | Did the offer persuade? |
| CVR | Conversions ÷ link clicks | Did the page close it? |
| CPA / CPL | Spend ÷ conversions | Is it profitable? |
| ROAS | Revenue ÷ spend (e-commerce) | Is it profitable? |
| Frequency | Impressions ÷ reach | Is the audience fatigued? |

**Warning:** some tools calculate hold rate against impressions, not 3-second plays. Always state the denominator in reports.

### 7.2 Benchmarks (cold prospecting, video)

| Metric | Kill | Fix | Good | Elite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook rate | Under 15% | 15 to 25% | 30 to 40% | 40%+ |
| Hold rate (15s ÷ 3s) | Under 10% | 10 to 20% | 25%+ | 30%+ |
| Link CTR (e-commerce) | Under 0.7% | 0.7 to 1.5% | 1.5%+ | 2.5%+ |

**Rules:**

- Read nothing before 2,000 impressions per ad.
- Compare like with like: same placement mix, same audience temperature, same video length.
- Replace these ranges with the account's own 90-day medians as soon as possible. Assumption: hook rate bands and the 25% hold / 1.5% CTR "good" marks come from published industry sources; the CTR kill and elite marks and hold rate fix band are Vendo working defaults, not sourced benchmarks. Lead-gen (dental) CTR norms differ from e-commerce; use account history.
- For statics, hook rate doesn't exist. Use CTR and CPM-adjusted CTR as the attention signal.
- Hook and hold are triage metrics, not success metrics. A 45% hook rate that doesn't convert is expensive attention.

### 7.3 Diagnostic tree

Work top to bottom. Fix the first broken stage before touching anything below it.

| Symptom | Stage broken | Likely copy cause | Fix | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low hook rate | Attention | Hook not relevant, too polished, wrong awareness level | New hooks (Section 4, Step 4); native formats; call out the audience | Caples, Hott, Schwartz |
| High hook, low hold | Interest | Hook promised something the body didn't deliver; slow middle | Tighten body; move the payoff earlier; add story or demonstration | Denney, Halbert |
| Good hold, low CTR | Persuasion | Weak offer, no proof, no reason to act now, unclear CTA | Add proof stack; strengthen offer; add risk reducer; one CTA | Cialdini, Bencivenga, Goff |
| Good CTR, low CVR | Conversion | Message mismatch with LP; LP lacks proof or detail; friction | Match LP headline to ad; add proof and FAQ; cut form fields | Ogilvy, Wiebe |
| Good CVR, rising CPA over time | Fatigue | Same angle overexposed; frequency climbing | New angles, not just new hooks | Sharp, Denney |
| Everything mediocre | Strategy | Wrong market, weak offer, no big idea | Go back to research and offer | Halbert, Albuquerque |
| Short-term fine, blended CAC rising | Brand gap | All activation, no demand creation | Add broad, emotional brand creative (aim nearer 60/40 over time) | Binet and Field |

### 7.5 Account-level checks

- [ ] **Angle diversity:** at least 3 distinct angles live (not 3 hooks on one angle). Meta's delivery system rewards genuinely different creative.
- [ ] **Awareness coverage:** cold ads for problem/solution aware; retargeting for product/most aware.
- [ ] **Lever coverage:** social proof and authority present; unity used for local businesses.
- [ ] **Brand vs activation:** some share of spend on emotional, non-offer creative.
- [ ] **Winner analysis:** top 3 ads broken down by hook type, angle, format, lever. Why did each win?
- [ ] **Loser analysis:** bottom 3 by spend. Which stage failed?
- [ ] **Refresh cadence:** new creative launched in the last 14 days.
- [ ] **Creative tracker up to date** with hypotheses and learnings.
- [ ] **Google RSAs:** ad strength Good or Excellent; keyword in headlines; no "Poor" assets left running.

## 8. Worked examples and testing cadence

The examples show the process applied end to end. All figures in brackets are placeholders to replace with verified client facts.

### 8.1 Dental: Invisalign, cold Meta, problem aware

**Brief:** big idea = "Straighten your smile discreetly, and see the result before you commit." Levers: social proof (primary), reciprocity (secondary). Mechanism: 3D scan preview.

**Weak version (fails 7 of 12 laws):**

```markdown
Transform your smile with our state-of-the-art Invisalign treatment! Our friendly team offers the best care. Book now!
```

Problems: no hook, vague adjectives, no proof, no mechanism, no risk reducer, sounds like every competitor.

**Strong version (PAS):**

```markdown
Still covering your mouth when you laugh?

Most of our Invisalign patients say the same thing: they'd stopped smiling in photos years ago.

A free 3D scan shows you your new smile before you spend a penny. Clear aligners, no metal, most treatments [X] months.

⭐ [4.9] stars from [1,200+] local patients

Free consultation. 0% finance available. No obligation.
👉 Book your scan
```

Why it works: VoC hook (Wiebe), emotion first (Binet and Field), mechanism (Georgi), proof (Cialdini), risk reversal (Kahneman), one CTA (Goff).

### 8.2 E-commerce: premium bell tent, retargeting, product aware

```markdown
Still thinking about it?

[X] families camped in one last summer.
⭐ "Survived a storm in Wales, bone dry" [verified review]
⭐ 100% cotton canvas, [X]mm groundsheet
⭐ Free UK delivery + [X]-day returns

Delivered in [X] days. 👉 Shop now
```

Levers: social proof and risk reversal. Proof stack framework. Specific, falsifiable claims only.

### 8.4 Testing cadence

| Frequency | Action | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly | Launch 3 to 5 new hooks on the best angle; kill ads under hook-rate kill line after 2,000 impressions | Paid social |
| Fortnightly | Launch 1 new angle (new research insight, new lever, or new format) | Paid social + creative |
| Monthly | Full audit (Section 7): scorecard top 10 ads, diagnostic tree, report | Account manager |
| Quarterly | Refresh VoC bank; re-check awareness mix; review brand vs activation split | Account lead |

**Test priority order:** angle, then hook, then format, then body, then CTA. Big levers first.
