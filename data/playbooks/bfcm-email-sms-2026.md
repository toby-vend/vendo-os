# BFCM & Q4 Email/SMS — Vendo Playbook

How Vendo plans Klaviyo email and SMS for Black Friday / Cyber Monday (BFCM) and the wider Q4, from September prep through Christmas and the January–February retention window. Source: Evernue, "The 2026 BFCM & Q4 Email & SMS Playbook" (Gamma, 2026) — distilled and Vendo-framed; Evernue's own sales/audit pitches removed. Original: https://gamma.app/docs/BFCM-Email-SMS-Playbook-2026-xsbukeqtlo3jhcd

**Core principle: BFCM is a system, not a campaign.** The offer sits at the centre; calendar, creative, SMS, segmentation, VIP/early access, pop-ups, welcome, abandonment and post-purchase flows, and QA/rollback all have to tell the same story at the same time.

## How to apply this at Vendo

- **Ecommerce clients where Vendo runs Klaviyo.** Full framework applies. Start the September/October prep (list health, deliverability, VIP list) before any offer is agreed. Check which channels Vendo actually manages for each client before planning.
- **Ecommerce clients where Vendo runs ads but not email (check per client).** Use Parts 2–4 to line paid social and search up with the client's offer windows and email/SMS moments: the same offer, the same dates, the same deadline messages.
- **Client-specific rules override this playbook.** Send caps, banned content, brand voice and approval lead times in a client's brief win over the cadence advice here. The "elevated/aggressive" cadence below is a proposal to put to the client's approver, never a default.
- **Dental clients.** Limited relevance. Private practices rarely run BFCM-style discounting, and any treatment promotion must meet GDC and ASA healthcare advertising rules. The useful parts are list hygiene, consent, the Q1 retention thinking (recall and treatment follow-up) and, at most, a seasonal event or open-day push using the same tease → launch → last-chance structure.
- **Plant hire clients.** Not applicable beyond general list-hygiene and consent rules.
- **Vendo standard: report BFCM results on Shopify revenue.** Klaviyo, Meta and Google each claim credit for the same orders, so attributed revenue double counts. Use platform attribution to compare sends against each other, and Shopify (ex VAT) for the total. Meta numbers use 7-day click attribution only.
- **Frequency is earned, not assumed.** Only push cadence up for clients whose engaged segment and deliverability are healthy going into November.
- **Every temporary BFCM change gets a written rollback** (date, owner, what reverts) before it goes live.

## The benchmark (why it matters)

Klaviyo's 2025 BFCM recap (global platform totals, reported in US currency; approx. GBP conversions shown, not a UK client benchmark): roughly £3bn of Klaviyo-attributed revenue over BFCM (+27% YoY), 22.7 billion messages sent (+25% YoY), and Black Friday was the platform's first single day above roughly £0.8bn. ## UK consent and promotion rules

The source is written for a US audience. In the UK the rules below apply to every send, at every cadence tier:

- **Consent (PECR and UK GDPR).** Marketing email and SMS to individuals needs prior opt-in consent, or the "soft opt-in": the person is an existing customer (or bought/negotiated to buy), the message is about similar products, and they were given a simple opt-out when their details were collected and in every message since. No pre-ticked boxes. Email consent does not cover SMS: collect SMS consent separately.
- **Every message carries an opt-out**, and SMS identifies the sender with a free or low-cost opt-out (e.g. reply STOP). Honour unsubscribes immediately and never import old or bought lists to boost BFCM volume.
- **Keep consent records** (source, wording, timestamp). Klaviyo stores these per profile; check pop-up and checkout wording before October list-growth pushes.
- **Send timing:** there is no UK legal quiet-hours rule for SMS, but avoid late-night and early-morning sends.
- **Urgency and pricing must be genuine.** Under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 the CMA can fine directly for misleading practices, including fake countdown timers, false scarcity ("only 2 left" when untrue) and misleading reference prices. "Was" prices must be genuine previous selling prices, and "up to X% off" claims need a fair share of the range at that discount (ASA/CAP rules). Deadlines in emails must match when the offer actually ends.
- **Prices are shown VAT-inclusive** to consumers, as standard UK practice.

## Part 1 — Prepare

**Review last Q4 first:** offers and timing, number of emails/SMS sent, top campaigns by revenue/clicks/conversions, when demand rose and fell, best products/categories/bundles, flow revenue, list growth. Key question: what do we repeat, change or drop?

**Decide what you are optimising for** — revenue, product focus (clearance/launch/priority collections), customer acquisition, existing customers, retention. BFCM is not the time to chase one perfect metric.

**Deliverability before volume:**
- Know the engaged audience (opening, clicking, buying) before increasing sends.
- Don't pull long-term inactives back into normal sending because BF is coming.
- Ramp volume up before the peak, not on the day.
- Watch clicks, unsubscribes, spam complaints and bounces as volume rises.
- Earn the right to send aggressively in BFCM before BFCM arrives.

**Grow the audience before the offer is live:** visitor → email/SMS subscriber (onsite forms); email → SMS subscriber; subscriber → BFCM VIP / early-access list.

**Segments to have ready:** engaged; VIP/high-value; previous BFCM buyers; recent browsers/abandoners; discount responders; lapsed customers; recent purchasers (to suppress from repeat reminders). Widen at the biggest launch and deadline moments, tighten on engagement in between.

## Part 2 — Offers

A good BFCM offer is **easy to understand, compelling vs the rest of the year, genuinely time-limited and commercially sensible**. It doesn't have to be a bigger discount. Mechanics: sitewide %, spend & save tiers, gift with purchase, bundles, free/upgraded shipping, BFCM-exclusive product, mystery offer, limited-quantity bonus.

**One offer vs several:** one offer over 2–3 weeks goes stale, forces repetitive angles, gives non-buyers no new reason and only creates two urgency peaks (launch and deadline). Multiple offers create several urgency peaks, re-engage non-buyers, invite repeat purchase and show what each audience responds to. Longer window → more distinct promotional moments.

**Build through Q4:** October early gifting (light/no discount) → early November early-bird → mid-November build-up/early access → Black Friday hero offer (biggest, simplest) → Cyber Monday refresh → December gifting & Christmas → Boxing Day/Q5 one more peak.

### Five November structures

| Option | Shape | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Black November | Main offer runs 3–4 weeks from ~1 Nov → BF → CM | Capturing demand early; comfortable promoting all month | Offer fatigue, repetitive messaging |
| 2. Early Bird + BFCM | Short early-bird, then main BFCM | Earlier revenue without running the main offer all month | Early bird and BFCM must use different mechanics |
| 3. Multiple early offers + BFCM | Gifting → offer 1/2/3 → BFCM → CM | Brands with many products/categories/angles | Needs real variety or it becomes discount fatigue |
| 4. BF Week + CM | Normal trading, then 5–7 day promo | Protecting early-November full-price trading | Frequency jumps sharply once live — plan it in advance |
| 5. BF Weekend + CM | Short, premium, high-urgency event | Brands that want minimal time discounting | Small revenue window; launch, reminder and last-chance sends are critical |

**Cyber Monday is a fresh reason to buy, not a deeper discount.** Either refresh the mechanic at similar value (BF 25% off → CM 20% off + free gift) or change the offer entirely (BF 25% sitewide → CM buy 2 get 1 free).

## Part 3 — Build hype

Before the main offer: teasers, VIP list building, product previews, wishlist/shopping prep, early access for the highest-intent audience. Aim: customers waiting for the sale, not stumbling on it.

**Stay visible without discounting all month.** Between promotional moments, send non-promotional content: best sellers, gift guides, reviews, new arrivals, product education, staff picks, brand story, customer stories, how-to, seasonal essentials, problem → solution, gifting inspiration.

## Part 4 — Execute

**One promotion, many angles:** tease → launch → product/benefit → social proof → objection handling → reminder → urgency → last chance.

**Creative rules:** offer is the hero; hierarchy is offer → supporting message → products/detail; one strong primary CTA; each email has one job; urgency visible; check on mobile first.

**Cadence tiers:** *standard* (evergreen) → *elevated* (approaching and during offers) → *aggressive* (biggest launch days, deadlines, final hours — multiple sends a day can be right). Frequency isn't what annoys a list; repeating the same message to the same people without relevance is.

| Day | Email | SMS |
|---|---|---|

| Tue | Product preview (best sellers, categories, wishlist) | — |
| Wed | Early access is live | Early access notification |
| Thu | Early access reminder — product or social-proof angle | — |
| BF AM | Black Friday is live — hero offer, primary CTA | BF launch |
| BF PM | What everyone's buying | — |
| Sat | Why buy now — benefits, reviews, objections | — |
| Sun | Still thinking about it — reminder + product angle | — |
| CM AM | Cyber Monday / offer refresh | — |

**Strategic resends** for the most important sends only — change subject line, preview text, creative/message or audience; never resend the identical email.

**SMS amplifies, it doesn't mirror email.** Use it for early access, launch, major offer change, genuine low stock, final hours. Send only to profiles with SMS-specific consent (see UK consent rules above).

## Part 5 — The whole Klaviyo account

**Pop-ups change with the promotion:** pre-BFCM VIP/early-access → BF live-offer pop-up → CM pop-up if offer/deadline changes → December gifting/evergreen. Pause evergreen discount forms that conflict with the live offer.

**Flows must know it's Black Friday:**
- Remove/pause conflicting evergreen incentives (welcome, winback) that undercut the BFCM offer.
- Pause emails with outdated offers or messaging.
- Add BFCM banners/content so people entering flows know what's live.
- Shorten abandonment delays during short promo windows.
- **Review first:** welcome, site/browse abandonment, added to cart, checkout abandonment, post-purchase. **Also check:** winback, replenishment, review request, cross-sell, back in stock, price drop, sunset/re-engagement, custom promo flows.

**Go further for high-volume clients:** BFCM-specific versions of welcome (offer impossible to miss), abandonment flows (intent + live offer + deadline, more direct as intent rises) and post-purchase (especially first-time buyers).

**Segmentation evolves mid-promotion:** suppress recent buyers from reminders for an offer they've used — but keep talking to them: thank, introduce the brand, educate, cross-sell, review request at the right time, second purchase.

**Rollback when an offer ends:** remove banners, restore flow timing, reactivate paused emails, restore evergreen pop-ups, remove expired codes, transition to CM/Christmas.

**Pre-launch QA:** codes, links, campaign and SMS schedules, segments and exclusions, pop-up dates, flow banners, paused vs active flow emails, mobile creative, deadlines, shipping info, rollback timing.

## Part 6 — Beyond BFCM

**December angles:** gifting, bundles (AOV), shipping-deadline urgency, last-minute gifts, gift cards after shipping cut-off, Boxing Day/Q5. Plan December alongside November, not after BF. A winning BFCM offer can return as an end-of-year offer.

**Three December approaches:** gifting/evergreen-led; one pre-Christmas promotion; multiple December promos. Pick based on brand, BFCM performance and appetite for discounting.

**Q1 retention:** December — strong post-purchase experience; January — build the relationship without leading with discounts (brand story, education, getting more from the purchase, reviews, complementary/new products); February onwards — chase the second purchase with cross-sell, replenishment or a winback incentive where justified.

## Checklist

**Before BFCM:** review last year · set Q4 goals · confirm November structure · lock offers and dates · review deliverability and list health · grow email/SMS list · build VIP/early-access audience · prepare segments · build calendar · plan cadence · plan SMS · build BFCM pop-ups · review and adjust flows · BFCM-specific flows where justified · confirm UK carrier shipping deadlines · check email and SMS consent wording and opt-outs · confirm every price claim and deadline is genuine · QA every campaign, code, link and segment.

**During:** monitor performance · raise cadence where earned · strategic resends · adjust targeting as people buy · keep flows and pop-ups aligned · SMS on high-intent moments · keep deadlines accurate.

**After:** remove expired messaging · restore evergreen flows and pop-ups · move into Christmas · shipping deadlines · gifting and gift cards · plan Boxing Day/Q5 · nurture BFCM customers to a second purchase.
