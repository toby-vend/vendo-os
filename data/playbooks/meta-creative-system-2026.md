# Meta Creative System 2026 — Vendo Playbook

How Vendo runs Meta creative as a system: how Andromeda distributes ads, how to read results by account level, hook craft, format economics, fatigue and portfolio management, and where AI belongs in production. Adapted from a two-hour ecommerce-focused talk by Blue Sense Digital, the same Australian performance agency behind the talk in the Meta Creative Strategy playbook. Source: Blue Sense Digital, "Meta Ads Creative Strategy in 2026: The Full System" ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vUbLw80KTpo)).

**Core principle: every ad will fatigue and every ad has a maximum spend, so run a portfolio, not heroes.** The creative is the targeting. Build diverse concepts, clear a known quality bar, launch as much volume above it as the budget supports, and judge ads at ad set and campaign level, never on ad-level ROAS.

**Relationship to the Meta Creative Strategy playbook:** that playbook already covers the basics (creative as the lever, persona × angle × offer, one concept per ad set, 10+ ads per concept, spend-first metric order, ABO vs CBO as a risk decision, content mix, production tiers). This playbook only summarises those briefly and focuses on what this talk adds or changes. Where the two talks disagree, both positions are stated with a Vendo working rule.

**Currency note:** the speaker says his figures are AUD and roughly hold in US currency too. Money figures below are converted to approximate GBP as if quoted in US currency (about £1 = 1.27, rounded), so the true GBP figure is probably lower still, or they are restated as ratios. Spend tiers, costs per ad and creator pay come from large Australian and US ecommerce accounts and are labelled **source figure, not a UK benchmark**. Never quote them to a client as what a UK account should achieve. Bullets marked **Vendo adaptation** or **Vendo standard** are ours; where a Vendo standard conflicts with the talk, the Vendo standard wins.

## How to apply this at Vendo

- **Dental (core vertical, private UK practices, high-ticket treatments).** The most useful new material for dental is: hooks that agitate the problem instead of calling out the persona; the bridge (bring the practice and treatment in late, after the viewer is problem- and solution-aware); long-form-copy statics and VSL-style education for older personas (implants, dentures); and retargeting as objection handling (price → monthly finance framed per day or per week, trust → real reviews, quality → real cases, not ready → education). Local catchments are small, churning audiences, so expect the "audience too small" fatigue driver early and rotate creative rather than pushing spend.
- **Vendo standard (dental measurement).** Meta's health-data rules block sending lead events back for private healthcare, so View Content is the optimisation event and lead proxy on dental website campaigns. It is a compliance constraint, not a learning-phase tactic. Read every "ROAS or CPA" instruction below as cost per lead within one objective; never compare instant-form CPL with View Content CPL.
- **Ecommerce (Shopify brands such as Veltuff, Sword Stall, MR Mouldings).** The whole talk applies. The new tools are: format economics (cost to make an ad vs contribution margin it earns), the DPA warning, incremental attribution as a check, shaping each winner's spend curve to inventory and cash, and portfolio management at product level. **Vendo standard:** anchor on Shopify revenue, not pixel-attributed ROAS, where they disagree. Veltuff reports in DKK; never mix its figures with GBP accounts.
- **Plant hire (local lead gen, B2B-leaning).** Treat as services. Personas are very specific and audiences tiny (a regional site manager hiring a 3-tonne excavator), so the small-audience spend cap bites quickly. Use problem-agitation and specific-proof hooks, and native local voices.
- **Vendo standard (attribution).** Every campaign is built on 7-day click only. The talk also endorses Meta's incremental attribution; use it only as a comparison column to sanity-check (Columns → Compare attribution settings), not as the campaign setting.
- **Media spend is client pass-through.** Clients pay Meta directly, so any advice to move a share of media budget into production is the client's money and decision. Propose it in £, never commit it.

## 1. Why creative, and the portfolio model

[0:00](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=0)

- Creative as the remaining lever is covered in the Meta Creative Strategy playbook. New here: **the portfolio model** ([2:45](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=165)). Three ads together holding a budget at a blended return always beat one ad holding the same budget at the same return. If the single hero fatigues, the account is done; with three, the others take the slack.
- **The cautionary pattern** ([3:51](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=231)): several businesses arrived down about 40% year on year in new-customer acquisition after building on three creatives that all fatigued together. By then the fix was six to eight months too late. The common cause: find one or two winners, stop making ads to save production cost, double down.
- **Three team mistakes** ([5:12](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=312)):
  1. **Confusing activity for strategy.** Accounts launching 2,000+ ads a month without a quality bar or strategy, unprofitable. Volume only works with a standard behind it.
  2. **Misdiagnosing bottlenecks** ([6:30](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=390)). Real creative bottlenecks: quality, format diversity, too few assets at a given awareness stage (usually top of funnel), and not using spend-unlocking tactics such as partnership ads.
  3. **Treating creative as a cost centre** ([7:50](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=470)). Platforms provide distribution; creative drives revenue.

## 2. How Andromeda distributes creative (what's new)

[9:05](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=545)

- **Andromeda reads the ad itself** (transcript, on-screen words, copy, visuals, historical conversion data) and finds the people for it. The speaker recommends broad targeting for nearly every account. Ads with no clear audience ("Hey everyone, look at this product") give Meta nothing to go on ([11:45](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=705)).
- **Creative similarity score** ([12:06](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=726)): Meta now scores how similar an account's creatives are (higher is worse). Most advertisers can't see it; a dedicated Meta rep can supply it.
- **Andromeda bundling** ([12:31](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=751)): slight variants (same shoot with the model re-posed, same UGC with a small script change, different formats saying the same thing to the same person) are bundled and served to one pool. Frequency rises, reach falls, returns decay.
- **Sequencing and the last-click trap** ([17:38](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=1058)): Meta optimises a multi-click and view-based sequence of ads but reports last click only. **Never switch off ads inside an ad set that is hitting KPI**, even the ones with poor ad-level numbers; Meta is funding them on purpose.
- **How far to trust results by level** ([20:43](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=1243)):

| Level | Reliability | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ad | Near-meaningless | Last-click credit over a multi-ad journey |
| Ad set | Fairly reliable | Judged as a group; some cross-serving between ad sets still happens |
| Campaign (7-day click, existing customers excluded) | Very reliable | Generally matches the P&L |

- **Hooks steer targeting** ([24:51](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=1491)): because every ad is auto-transcribed, a call-out such as "If you're a business owner turning over £4M a year" (approx.) sends Andromeda looking for that audience. If the rest of the ad isn't for them, you've pushed it to the wrong people.
- **Format variety reaches different people** ([23:29](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=1409)): Meta shows people the formats they engage with. An account running only UGC and DPAs never reaches someone who never engages with either.
- **Most ads have low spend capacity**: the mean spend per ad is roughly £550–£1,200 (approx.; source figure, not a UK benchmark). Volume compensates.
- **Vendo adaptation:** for dental, a "Bournemouth! If your dentures slip…" style location + pain opener is still fine: location is genuinely targetable and the pain half agitates. What to avoid is call-outs on things Meta can't target (income, job seniority) and call-outs with no pain in them. Confirm the ad's target areas with the practice; they often differ from the practice's own town.

## 3. Concepts: what's new on personas and angles

[26:08](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=1568)

- Concept = persona + angle + offer (see the Meta Creative Strategy playbook). New: **format is a fourth, weaker lever** post-Andromeda; it reaches some new people but mostly the same ones ([41:32](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=2492)).
- **Persona includes who appears in the ad** ([26:56](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=1616)): a 60-year-old on screen reaches a different audience from a 20-year-old. **Vendo standard:** dental statics lead with lifestyle imagery cast to the persona's age; never AI-generated people in scrubs or fake dentists.
- **Build many personas, then expand with AI** ([29:36](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=1776)): the speaker builds 30–40 good personas by hand, gives an LLM very wide context, asks for ~20 more, keeps the best 4–5, feeds them back and asks again. With thin input, AI returns the average.
- **Specificity beats breadth** ([31:18](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=1878)): "a consultant who flies twice a week and needs a carry-on wardrobe for boardrooms and bars", not "busy professional"; "a mum who's tried six supplements because her toddler won't eat vegetables", not "health-conscious parent". Broad-unaware ads hold more spend in theory, but are very hard to make well; leave them to very large brands.
- **The angle toolkit** ([33:23](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=2003)): problem agitation, contrarian truth, social proof, domain authority (fit the domain: a marathon runner for a running product, not a lab coat), curiosity gap, comparison/objection handling ("tried this, didn't work…"), and transformation (before and after).
- **Vendo standard (before and afters):** the speaker says Meta dislikes before/afters and you should "sneak them past the algorithm". Vendo does not try to evade Meta review. Use transformations only where Meta's policies and UK rules allow (ASA/CAP Code, GDC guidance for dental), with real patients and consent.
- **Worked concept** ([45:38](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=2738)): a male gym-goer training for a sub-4-hour marathon, earning around £55K (approx.) → angle: science-backed recovery for the long run, about 5 minutes off marathon time → offer: buy two get one free (raising perceived value rather than cutting price) → about 10 ads across UGC, founder talking head, educational piece and carousel. Swap to women training for a marathon, or to couch-to-5K beginners, and each is a new concept.

### Testing priority: where the two talks differ

| Talk | Order |
| --- | --- |
| This talk (ecommerce, 2026) | **Angle** → Offer → Persona → Format ([38:21](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=2301)) |
| Meta Creative Strategy playbook talk | Ecommerce: Persona → Offer → Angle → Format. Services: **Offer** → Persona → Angle → Format |

This talk's reasoning: if a *proven* buyer persona isn't buying, the problem is the message (angle) or the offer, not the persona; changing persona just points bad ads at different people. Offers in ecommerce hit a wall quickly (after a few bundle and value-add variations, only deep discounting is left, which eats margin and trains discount reliance).

**Vendo standard: test angle first in every vertical (dental, ecommerce, plant hire), then hook, format, body and CTA, as set out in the Vendo Copywriter SOP.** The source's offer-first and persona-first orders are the presenter's views, not Vendo practice.

## 4. Hooks: grading, types and the bridge

[48:16](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=2896)

- A hook is a play for relevance, not a trick. Three layers: visual, audio, copy. About 80% of viewers never get past it.
- **Spend effort on hooks, not bodies** ([49:33](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=2973)): an hour making 2 bodies + 15 hooks nearly always beats 2 hooks + 5 bodies.
- **Grade every hook on five criteria** ([49:58](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=2998)); a couple done well is enough:
  1. **Clarity** — a stranger understands it within 3 seconds.
  2. **Relevance** — agitate the actual problem; persona call-outs are overrated.
  3. **Novelty** — "purple ocean": new angle on proven demand. Test: "has this hook been done a million times?"
  4. **Specificity** — numbers, names, quantified outcomes.
  5. **Credibility** — usually carried by the visual (a relevant expert or recognisable face).
- **Nine hook types** ([54:25](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=3265)):

| Type | Example shape | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem agitation | "Struggling with back pain from desk work?" | Problem-aware only; doesn't scale beyond that stage |
| Contrarian truth | "Everything you've been told about X is wrong" | Works across awareness levels |
| Specific proof | "Lost 12 kilos in 90 days without giving up pasta" | Credibility inside the hook |
| Curiosity gap | "What the top 1% know about X that you don't" | Close the gap late |
| Truth bomb | "This costs about £95. Yes, it's expensive. Here's why it's our best seller" (approx.) | Premium products where price is the main objection |
| Psychological confrontation | "That drawer full of products you never use? That's the problem." | Feels like a friend calling you out |
| Sensory / ASMR | 2–3 wordless seconds of texture, pouring, unboxing | Strong in fashion right now |
| Founder's letter | "I remortgaged my house to fund the first batch…" | Must be true; real stakes are why it works |
| Social proof | "90% of customers saw results in 30 days" | Lead with a credibility marker |

- **The bridge** ([59:02](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=3542)) is the transition from hook to body and body to CTA. A great hook followed by an instant product pitch ("…Well, [brand] creatine is your solution") ruins the ad. Move viewers through the awareness stages (unaware → problem aware → solution aware → product aware) and **introduce the product as late as possible** ([1:02:30](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=3750)). Don't plan product placement around retention curves; a later reveal gives fewer but far higher-intent viewers and more total purchases.
- **A new hook is a new creative to Meta** ([1:03:52](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=3832)): it gets its own creative ID and isn't bundled with the original. It's almost free: record each hook several times on the shoot day, or put new hooks on fatigued winners. The speaker's team pushed roughly another £80K (approx.) through fatigued ads at the same efficiency with about an hour of new hooks. Bad-hook-rate ads can be revived the same way.
- **Vendo adaptation:** on every dental and ecommerce shoot, script and record 3–6 alternative hooks per body; they are the cheapest volume we can make. Name each hook in the ad name's Detail field (see Vendo naming standard below). For UGC, hooks track what the creator actually says.

## 5. Formats: choosing, economics, DPAs and partnership ads

[1:04:46](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=3886)

- **Five questions, format last**: what's the concept? does it need education (longer form)? can the value fit one frame (static/carousel)? does it need a demo? what awareness stage?
- **Statics are underrated** ([1:08:21](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=4101)). "Statics only work at the bottom of the funnel" is a skill issue; some accounts run very large daily budgets on top-of-funnel statics alone (source figure: hundreds of thousands a day, not a UK benchmark). Two uses: rapid-test angles with statics then develop winners into video, or lean hard on statics because they're cheap.
- **Format economics** ([1:06:04](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=3964)): compare the **cost to make an ad** with the **average contribution margin each ad earns**, split by format. In the speaker's example the average video out-earned the average static, but statics cost a fraction as much and returned several times their cost, while videos returned less than they cost to make, so the account doubled down on statics. The figures are source figures, not UK benchmarks; the method is what to copy.
- **Three static styles that work** ([1:08:47](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=4127)):
  1. **Organic-feeling post format** (e.g. a reposted tweet listing symptoms → causes) run through an expert's own profile: wide problem agitation plus education, no brand on the post.
  2. **Advertorial card** linking to a "best products of the year" review page. **Vendo standard:** don't replicate the mechanic in the speaker's example (a brand-owned "independent" review site where only the brand's product is clickable). Under the UK CAP Code, ads must be obviously identifiable as ads and comparisons must be fair and verifiable. A genuine, clearly branded educational landing page is fine.
  3. **Long-form-copy static** ([1:12:54](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=4374)): one image plus a long story that walks a cold, older (50+) audience from unaware to product aware. Needs real copywriting skill.
- **VSLs** ([1:13:45](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=4425)) perform well with older demographics because they have room to teach. The speaker's examples are US/AU health supplements; their health claims would not all be permitted in UK advertising, so copy the structure (problem → education → objection handling → mechanism → proof → product late), not the claims.
- **Vendo adaptation:** long-form-copy statics and VSL-style talking heads suit dental implant and denture personas (older, high consideration). The clinician on camera must be the practice's real dentist. AI can help produce statics and edits, but **Vendo standard:** never AI-generated clinicians or patients, no cartoon mascots (animated ads are motion-graphics explainers).
- **DPA warning** ([1:22:17](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=4937)) — ecommerce: dynamic product ads look great on attributed ROAS, but strip out existing customers and check incremental attribution and they often fall by about half. Moving budget from "poor" top-of-funnel ads into DPAs starves the funnel and the account topples. DPAs can work cold at small daily budgets but don't scale. **Vendo standard:** check DPA performance against Shopify new-customer revenue before shifting budget.
- **Funnel allocation now lives in production, not campaigns** ([1:24:33](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=5073)): roughly 70–90% of assets aimed at top of funnel, about 20% middle, 5–10% bottom (the speaker's ranges don't sum exactly; read it as "most production goes to cold audiences").
- **Partnership ads** ([1:25:43](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=5143)): run through a creator's profile with both handles; targeting uses both audiences, and they feel less like ads. Some of the speaker's accounts put up to 40% of spend through them. Meta can now split-test brand-only, creator-only and both.

## 6. Testing, structure and metrics (what's new)

[1:26:55](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=5215)

- **Cycle:** hypothesis (a concept with one variable changed, plus expected spend and results) → build a batch → launch → analyse against the hypothesis → scale winners → iterate. 10+ ads to test a concept properly (hit rate ~5%); see the Meta Creative Strategy playbook.
- **Account complexity should match business complexity** ([1:28:03](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=5283)). A small single-product account needs a very simple structure. Ads per ad set: minimum 3; worry about the lower limit, not the upper.
- **Don't add ads to a winning ad set** ([1:30:15](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=5415)): it partly resets learning and breaks sequencing. New ads for a winning concept go in a new ad set ("concept one — shoot two"). Underperforming ad sets can be changed freely.
- **Scaling a winner** ([1:31:31](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=5491)): raise budget; **never switch a winner off to relaunch it elsewhere**; optionally move post IDs into a scaling campaign (works in some accounts, not others). A separate retargeting campaign handles existing customers so cold campaigns can exclude them, plus an optional mid-funnel ad set on 90-day site visitors for long journeys.
- **Vendo standard (naming):** `<Ad set concept> | Format | Talent | Detail | YYMMDD`, e.g. a second shoot for a winning concept gets its own ad set and its ads keep the concept name with the new launch date. No "Copy" suffixes, no "-" placeholders.
- **Metric order** is the same as the Meta Creative Strategy playbook (amount spent → ROAS/CPA → CPC/CTR → hook/hold). New detail:
  - **CPC as a kill threshold** ([1:34:02](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=5642)): if no ad above a certain CPC has ever been profitable in *this* account, use that account's own threshold to cut ads early. Never borrow another account's figure.
  - **Hook rate** (share past 3 seconds) and **hold rate** (share past ~15 seconds) are diagnostics: bad hook rate → swap the hook; bad hold rate → fix the bridge.
- **ABO vs CBO** ([1:35:19](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=5719)): Pareto compounds, so about 4% of ads hold about 64% of spend and revenue. A CBO left alone funnels budget to that 4%, which is efficient but leaves no backups. The speaker runs ABO in steady state and switches to CBO temporarily for seasonal efficiency spikes. **Vendo adaptation:** this fits Vendo's ABO-testing + CBO-scaling builds; for peak periods (e.g. Black Friday on ecommerce) a temporary CBO push is reasonable if the testing pipeline keeps running.
- **Pain vs desire mix** ([1:38:14](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=5894)): brands built on campaign shoots often run ~90/10 desire-led to pain-led. Shift towards 60/40, the best closer to 70/30, pain-led.
- **Retargeting = objection handling** ([1:39:11](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=5951)). Of the people who don't buy, assume a spread of price, trust, quality-doubt and not-ready objections, and make bottom-of-funnel creative for each:

| Objection | Creative answer | Vendo dental version |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | Value anchoring, cost-per-use ("the price of a coffee") | Monthly finance per day or week; never invent finance terms |
| Trust | Customer counts, real reviews, testimonials | Real Google reviews, patient stories with consent |
| Quality doubt | Product in use, demos, before/afters | Real cases within ASA/CAP and GDC rules; the clinician explaining the process |
| Not ready | Stay present with education or lifestyle content | Treatment explainers, open-day reminders |

## 7. Volume and the money model (what's new)

[1:41:47](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=6107)

- **Hierarchy** in this talk: volume → concept quality → hook → editing → UGC talent → storytelling (slightly different from the other talk's order; both put volume above the quality bar first).
- **Nobody can pick winners above the bar** ([1:42:41](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=6161)). Define the bar (say 7/10), clear it every time, then maximise volume.
- **Volume rule of thumb:** one new ad per month per roughly £800 of monthly spend (approx.; the speaker says his figure is in AUD, so the GBP equivalent is lower still). Vendo rounds to about one per £1,000 as a rough guide. Better: back-propagate (average spend per ad × average return = expected revenue per ad; target new-customer revenue ÷ that = ads needed). New caveats ([1:46:28](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=6388)): allow for **time-to-spend** (an ad's value can take months to materialise) and forecast **winners and losers separately**. The creative volume curve should mirror the revenue curve through the year.
- **Production budget — the two talks differ:** this talk says about **25% of media budget** in the mid tier, falling to about 10% at the top tier; the other talk says about 10% in the mid tier. Treat 10–25% as the range the agency recommends at large spend levels. **Vendo adaptation:** most Vendo clients spend well below these tiers; propose production in £ and as a share of the client's budget, and never commit it.
- **Cost-centre case study** ([1:49:35](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=6575)): a large account (source: roughly £275K a month in Meta spend, approx.) had only ~15 live ads and 25 over-segmented campaigns. Relaunching and reworking old performers took it to ~60 live ads with no new creative: CAC fell 20–30% and new-customer revenue rose about 28%. The founder then refused to move about 3% of media into production, the 60 ads fatigued and the account fell back. Lesson: media buying can only be pushed so far; volume is the bottleneck.
- **Trial reels** ([1:53:41](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=6821)): post ad cuts (CTA trimmed) as Instagram trial reels on the page the ad will run from. You get comparative reach and retention graphs showing whether the hook, bridge or body failed, without paying to test in Ads Manager. Use it to shortlist ~5 of ~20 hooks before launch. The speaker flags this as a current tactic that may change.
- **Vendo adaptation:** trial reels publish to the client's own Instagram page, so get the client's agreement before posting, and never post unapproved claims or offers.

## 8. Fatigue and portfolio management

[1:55:17](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=6917)

- **Every ad has a total spend capacity (the area under its spend curve), but you can shape the curve** ([1:56:44](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=7004)): squeeze it hard and it dies in weeks; run it gently and it can live for months. The right shape depends on **inventory and cash**: lots of stock to clear → squeeze; low stock or cash-constrained growth → let it breathe.
- **Agency warning** ([1:57:35](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=7055)): agencies tend to squeeze winners for a strong 30–90-day case study and efficiency reports, which can wreck longevity and inventory planning. **Vendo adaptation:** for ecommerce, ask about stock and cash position before deciding how hard to push a winner; this is part of the job, not an extra.
- **Two fatigue drivers** ([1:58:23](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=7103)): the ad reaches its lifetime capacity (faster if it shares visuals, concept and audience with other ads), or **the audience is too small**. A specific persona has a hard daily spend cap; find it, hold there, and rotate creative because the audience **churns** (people enter and leave the need). Don't drive frequency up until the audience stops being profitable.
- **Vendo adaptation (dental and plant hire):** a practice's catchment or a hire depot's radius is exactly this kind of small, churning audience. Hold spend near capacity and rotate creative; see the Meta Frequency & Creative Fatigue playbook for the frequency checks.
- **Four responses once an ad fatigues** ([2:01:36](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=7296)): new hooks on the hero ad; a cost-cap campaign (pushes spend but burns it faster); duplicating ad sets via post ID (not recommended, rarely saves anything); rotating formats (video → static, VSL → UGC, static → long-form-copy static). None does much once an ad is truly done, which is why you run a portfolio.
- **Balance sheet vs P&L** ([2:04:14](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=7454)) — ecommerce: optimising only for profit can leave cash tied up in unsold stock. Sometimes the account should be restructured to sell through specific stock at lower efficiency, with creative made for those products. The media buyer must know the inventory position.

## 9. Production: content types, briefing, team and AI

[2:05:31](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=7531)

- **Content mix:** about 50–60% replicating winners (hook iterations, format translations), 20–30% iterating underperformers or adapting working concepts to new personas/offers, 20–30% net-new concepts. Never 0% net new.
- **Briefing creators** ([2:07:48](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=7668)): output quality follows brief quality. When sourcing a creator, ask for past work **and the brief behind each piece**. If a creator wants to go off script, shoot one on-script and one natural take.
- **Team for volume** ([2:09:01](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=7741)): AI-native video editor, statics designer, creative strategist (hire a copywriter, poach one, or grow one from a designer or editor). The speaker mentions strategists paid a share of ad spend on their ads (around 3% on top of salary); that is an AU/US brand-side practice, not a Vendo recommendation.
- **Where AI fits** ([2:11:20](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=7880)): ideation → briefing → production → publishing → analysis. **Keep humans at the edges (ideation, analysis); use AI heavily in the middle (briefing, scripting, production).** A brand-specific script skill driven by a ~20-question brief (persona, concept, offer, length, awareness stage) gets scripts most of the way. AI analysis misses metric hierarchy, structure and commercial context.
- **Vendo standard (AI production):** AI-assisted statics and edits are fine within the brand rules: no AI clinicians or patients, no fabricated reviews, results or prices, products shown as new unless the brief says otherwise, and realism checked before anything goes live.
- **Organic first** ([2:14:05](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=8045)): posting organically trains the creative skill that compounds into paid.
- **Native creators** ([2:14:59](https://youtu.be/vUbLw80KTpo?t=8099)): creators must sound native to the target market; the same script needs a local voice. **Vendo adaptation:** UK accents for UK clients (ideally regional for local dental and plant hire); Danish creators for Veltuff's Danish market.
- **Copywriting reading list:** *Breakthrough Advertising* (Schwartz), *Scientific Advertising* (Hopkins), *Ogilvy on Advertising*, *Influence* (Cialdini).

Apply the bottom-tier logic: few concepts, script strength does the work, the included shoot day is used to capture as many hooks and takes as possible, and statics carry volume between shoots. Larger ecommerce accounts may reach the second tier.
